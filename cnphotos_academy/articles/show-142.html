Make Photo Shoot Fun: Record Every Moment of Your Child's Growth

Published: 2023-05-26

Source link: https://www.cnphotos.net/academy/show-142.html

Children are always photogenic and also famously hard to shoot. During a shoot, they may act shy or different from usual, which makes the photos look forced and unnatural, or at least hard to get.

A natural, candid photo of a child can be a challenge, but if you succeed, that photo may be treasured for years. To raise your chance of success, here are some tips to try when you shoot with children on the weekend.


- Make the shoot fun and playful -

We want children to have fun, because then they relax and are more likely to give you their most genuine smile. As a bonus, parents are happier too, because we are creating happy memories for their child.

How to make the shoot fun depends on the age and personality of the child. Prepare in advance based on their interests, and if you can, let them choose the props and the location. Letting them take part like this makes the shoot more meaningful and fun for them.

- Plan and style it well -

For older children who can take direction, you can plan and design a themed shoot. Effort and careful planning usually pay off with creative, fun, and beautiful images. See the following styled children's photos by Pye Jirsa.


- Go outdoors and discover the world -

Natural behavior is not a button you can turn on or off. If you tell a child to smile or to play, they may just cling to their parent with a blank face.

Instead of relying on words and obedience, choose a comfortable setting for the shoot. For many children, a fun outdoor place naturally brings out the expressions and behavior you want. A forest, a park, or a beach are all good choices, and the background will be beautiful.

- Bring the child's favorite toy -

If you cannot go out, you will rely on toys to make the child feel at ease, and the best is their favorite toy. Even if the toy is ugly or hard to shoot, it comforts the child. It is worth carrying just in case.

- Do not forget the classic props -

You should bring their favorite toy, but sometimes it does not suit the shot. You can use fun, photogenic props, such as pinwheels, bubble solution, or a magnifying glass.

- Step away -

Nothing kills play faster than a hovering adult. Do not tell the child what to do or not to do. Let them play in their own nature.

If the child does not act as you expect or hope, treat it as a chance to discover their personality. We often want to see exuberant joy and happiness, but some children are more thoughtful and reserved even when they play. They may not smile at the camera, but they still show other emotions worth shooting, such as curiosity.

- Be ready for lots of movement -

If the child you are shooting is still playing quietly, preparing for more movement will not hurt. Children never stay still. There may be calm moments, but the strongest emotions and the best shots often come right after they start running.

- Follow the child's lead -

Remember that we are not trying to control the child's behavior and personality, but to capture it truthfully in a photo.

In other words, you are an observer. If you need to give direction, it should be subtle and fun, not command-like. Unless they are about to damage something valuable such as your camera, stay relaxed and be open to what happens. You may find their creativity and originality more inspiring than anything you imagined.

- Ask if the child wants to pose -

Some children really enjoy posing. Even if the child seems not to like it, you can still ask if they want to pose, perhaps after you have taken some nice photos.

Sometimes, asking a child to pose for a photo is more comfortable than suddenly telling them to. Many children are now used to camera phones and spontaneous, unexpected shots, so being asked is a refreshing change that gives them a sense of control, which feels comforting.

If they say no, you quickly learn that posing will not work. That way, you do not waste time trying to make a child act happy for the camera when they would rather play.
