Photo Composition: Place the Subject and Supporting Elements Well, and Use Foreground and Background to Set Them Off

Published: 2021-10-25

Photography is not random snapping. It joins technique and art. To express the theme of a photo fully, the photographer must place every element of the frame and arrange the whole scene. The basic elements of composition are the subject, the supporting element, the foreground, the background, and empty space.

1. The subject:

The subject is the main thing the photo shows. It leads the whole photo, carries the main idea, and plays the leading role in the frame. It is the main reason the photographer creates the work.

The photographer can use rule of thirds, diagonal, S-curve, triangle, or frame composition to set off the subject.

2. The supporting element

The supporting element is the object that plays a supporting role in the photo. It sets off the subject, helps show the subject's traits and meaning, and builds the complete theme together with the subject.

When you choose a supporting element, check that it does not hurt the subject, and that it explains, sets off, or beautifies. To keep it from competing with the subject, let it appear incomplete, or leave it out of the frame so it only suggests itself to the viewer.

3. The foreground

The foreground is what sits in front of the subject and closest to the viewer. Trees and flowers usually serve as the foreground, but it can also be a person or an object.

When you show a foreground, it must relate to the theme. A framed foreground strengthens the pattern of the frame and makes it feel more whole and self-contained. A decorative foreground does this even more, and it helps draw the viewer's attention to the subject.

4. The background

The background is everything behind the subject. It sets the mood and sets off the subject. Its role is clear. It helps show the place and character of the subject, deepens and enriches the theme, and strengthens the real feeling in the frame.

The background should help the theme. If it has no clear meaning, remove it or weaken it. The background must contrast in tone with the foreground, subject, and supporting element, so the layers show.

5. Empty space

Empty space is the bare, object-free part of the frame. It is the part that is not a concrete object, but sets off the concrete ones. Empty space is not always pure white or black. Any part of the frame with a similar tone, a single tone, and the role of setting off the subject counts as empty space.

Empty space sets off and shows the subject, links the subject and the supporting element, and carries the mood of the frame. When you place empty space, first watch the direction the subject faces. Also watch the ratio of empty space to object, and build the feeling that "leave a third of the painting white, and the spirit rises with it."

Photo composition starts with placing the subject and supporting element well, and using the foreground and background to enrich the layers. It gives you works that have a foundation, technique, and thought.
