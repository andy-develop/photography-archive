7 Easy Ways to Take More Interesting Portrait Photos on Your Phone

Published: 2023-02-24

Source link: https://www.cnphotos.net/academy/show-119.html

Do you want to take great portraits with your phone? Here are 7 easy ways to try more interesting and engaging portraits.


1. Shoot with high-contrast light

If you want better portraits, shoot in high-contrast light.

High-contrast light means the scene has both bright areas and dark shadows.

So where can you find high-contrast light?

A sunny day is the best time to find this light. Go outside and find a building or wall where bright light meets dark shadow.

Then place part of your subject in the shadow.

Tap your phone screen to set focus on the subject's face. Then swipe down to lower the exposure, so the shadows look dark and striking.

Photos shot in high-contrast light look great in black and white.

You can also catch contrast light indoors. Find a dim room where soft light comes in through a window.

Have the subject stand near the window, so half of their face is lit by the window and the other half is in shadow.

If needed, lower the exposure so the shadows look dark and striking.

Strong light and shadow will create a beautiful portrait.

2. Get creative with light patterns

Light patterns also let you take interesting portraits.

Indoors, light falls in patterns when it passes through blinds or slanted glass.

Outdoors, you can also find patterns when light passes through leaves or fence slats.

You can also get creative by placing different objects in front of the light source. For example, you can use a filter to create a unique light pattern on your subject's face.

In most cases, place the subject so the light pattern falls on their face.

If the pattern glows on the wall, have the subject stand against the wall to add visual interest.

If needed, tap the phone screen to focus on the subject's face, then swipe down to lower the exposure.

Lowering the exposure keeps the subject's skin from looking too bright. Deep, dark shadows add drama to your photo.

Move the subject around in the light and try different poses. For example, you can catch a pattern on one side of their face, or use the light to highlight a feature such as their eyes.

3. Try different poses

Your subject's pose can make or break the photo. If you want to avoid dull poses, you need a little creativity.

Here are three simple poses that add fun and drama to your portraits.

The first pose is very easy. Just have the subject use their hands to cover their face.

Place one hand higher on one side of the face. Then place the other hand near the chin, gently touching the face.

This pose creates a natural frame that draws attention to the subject's face. It also helps them feel more relaxed during the shoot.

The next pose adds more emotion and drama. Have the subject tilt their head back and rest a hand gently on their neck.

This pose creates a wonderful, sensual mood. It is great for showing your subject's confidence and beauty.

Now, if you want a more unique portrait, try shooting from a low angle. Lower your phone to the ground and shoot up toward your subject.

Use the wide-angle lens on your phone and move close to the subject's feet, so they kick toward the lens.

Showing your subject from an angle people do not usually see will definitely get attention.

4. Create a dreamy mood

We have talked about adding drama to your photos. But how do you create a dreamy mood in a portrait?

It is easier than you think.

Focus on three things:

Motion, a relaxed dreamy expression, and elegant arm and hand placement.

A good way to add motion to your photo is to have your subject wear loose fabric or a long dress.

Once they are in place, set up your camera and have them toss the dress or fabric up. Then take a few photos as it falls.

To maximize your chance of a perfect shot, use burst mode on your phone camera.

The camera keeps shooting until you release the shutter button. Then you pick the best frame from the burst and discard the rest.

This unique way of catching falling fabric is perfect for a dreamy mood in a portrait.

Next, consider the subject's facial expression. A simple way to build a calm, dreamy mood is to have the subject relax their face and close their eyes.

Then you can raise the dreamy pose to a new level with elegant arm placement. Have the subject raise their hands above their head, with the elbows slightly bent.

Do not be afraid to be creative. Have them slowly move their hands and arms to see what works best.

5. Create fun photos with two people

Posing a portrait of two people can be challenging, whether they are a couple, siblings, or friends.

But once you know a few tricks, it is very easy.

Let us start simple. Have the two subjects stand side by side, with their faces on the same level.

Then move closer, so only half of each person's face is in the frame. This is a great way to capture the detail of a close-up of siblings.

If the subjects are different heights, have them stand at different distances from the camera.

Have the taller person stand closer to the camera facing one way, and have the shorter subject step back a few paces facing the opposite way. This gives the photo strong layers.

This angle is more interesting than having them stand side by side. You can also try blurring the background or the foreground.

If the near subject is very close to the camera, they will fall slightly out of focus. You can shoot in portrait mode on your phone to create shallow depth of field.

For a couple's portrait, you can try some of these simple poses.

Do not have your subjects look at the camera. Instead, have them look into the distance while they embrace. This builds a thoughtful mood in the photo.

Another option is to have them close their eyes and hug each other. This pose creates a strong emotional bond in the portrait.

You can try other poses too. Do not be afraid to experiment and be creative.

6. Use props to tell a story

One of the most fun ways to be creative in portrait photography is to tell a story.

Using props is the easiest way to add a storytelling element to a photo.

The props you can use are endless. Just make sure the prop helps tell the story you want to tell.

For example, you can use flowers as a prop to tell a story about love or sorrow.

You can have your subject hold the flowers, or place the flowers in the frame.

If you want to tell a story from an old era, look for vintage props, such as an old car and period clothing from that time.

Props such as books or a coffee cup give a sense of comfort.

If you want a different way to express inner emotion, have your subject wear a blindfold. In the photo below, I even added a few flowers to the blindfold for creativity.

As you can see, props add a whole new element to a photo. Add a few props, and you can take so many interesting photos with your phone.

7. Choose clothing to emphasize the theme

Your subject's clothing matters more than you think.

Clothing can tell a story or emphasize a theme, and it has a big effect on the overall look of the image.

So when you plan a portrait shoot, think carefully about what you want your subject to wear.

Try to match their clothing to the theme or the location, or vice versa.

For example, if you shoot in nature, clothing with floral patterns or neutral tones works well.

In an urban setting, stylish or grungy clothing is the best choice.

For a cozy mood, consider soft fabrics, a wool sweater, a shawl, or a blanket.

To add a touch of glamour, a shiny or sequined outfit is always a safe choice.

Also consider how the color of the clothing interacts with the setting.

Matching the clothing color to the surroundings creates a harmonious, pleasing look.

That said, do not be afraid to use a clothing color that contrasts with the background.

Of course, the clothing should also reflect the subject's personality. You can let the subject help choose the outfit.

Remember, choosing clothing and props for your theme does not have to be hard. Sometimes a simple hat or a flowing piece of fabric is enough.

These are the 7 ways to shoot portraits on your phone. Give them a try.
