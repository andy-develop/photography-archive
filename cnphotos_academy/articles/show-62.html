What Kind of Work Suits Black and White Tones?

Published: 2022-06-15

Does black and white make up for a lack of class? The appeal of black and white tones has not faded in the digital age. The reason is not class. It is that black and white is a minimal art with real appeal. Few people can make black and white photos interesting, because they have not mastered the skills of shooting black and white, such as how to choose a scene.

First, understand one point:

What are the strengths of black and white tones?

Black and white avoids color interference and expresses a simple image. Black and white is the art of subtraction in photography, but it is much harder to do well than color.

Black and white has a plain feel, and it is also a kind of fashion beauty.

Black and white can freeze time and space, giving endless room for imagination.

So what scenes suit black and white photos most? In what cases do we use black and white? For different subjects (landscape, portrait, documentary), what shooting methods are involved?

1. Buildings with strong outlines

Black and white mainly brings out the texture of light and shadow, so it suits objects with strong outlines.

2. Contrast of black and white

Black and white can be understood simply as the contrast of dark and light, and of black and white.

Large areas of white or black space express a simple idea.

3. Backlight

If you shoot a similar scene in strong backlight and do not fill light on the person, the color image looks dull gray and lacks color detail. But backlight suits black and white best, and it can show the backlit mood well.

Sometimes an image does not need much color detail. It only needs clear outline lines.

4. Strong contrast of light and dark

Normally we do not suggest shooting in strong light with a large light ratio. But for black and white photos, such an environment gives better results, because a scene with strong light and shadow contrast adds drama to the image.

When shooting portraits, you use a large light ratio to darken the background and bring out the texture of the face, to convey a weathered and resolute feeling.

5. Low-light, high-ISO scenes

When the light is dim and the camera has low dynamic range and poor high ISO, the shot has much noise and the image quality suffers. In black and white mode, some of that noise adds texture instead.

6. Landscape

Black and white technique is also often used for long exposures in landscape photography. Such a shot needs an ND1000 filter and an exposure time of one minute or more.

8. Humanistic

Black and white humanistic subjects are usually shot in a large light ratio environment. You use the shadow of the light to bring out the contrast of light and dark, or choose something like a zebra crossing with light and dark contrast.
