He Waited Three Years for One Photo. No Successful Work Comes from a Snap!

Published: 2022-05-06

Many girls hope to have a professional photographer as a friend. They look smooth and seem to take photos with no effort, and they shoot many beautiful, moving photos.

In fact, others never know how much effort these photographers put in behind the photo. Time, preparation, luck, and patience, all of this is their cost.

Albert Dros, the famous Dutch landscape photographer, is an official NiSi collaborating photographer.

His work has been published by some of the world's largest media outlets, including Time, The Huffington Post, the Daily Mail, and National Geographic.

He is skilled at high-quality landscape photos. When he shares this work online, most viewers love it, and it wins endless praise.

Albert Dros lives in the Netherlands, and he shoots stunning photos in his homeland.

When we speak of the Netherlands, we must speak of its national flower, the tulip. The tulip is a symbol of beauty, dignity, luxury, and success.

You can shoot tulips in the Netherlands every year, but the sea of flowers looks different each time. To shoot one photo he was happy with, he waited a full three years.

He believes that to shoot good photos, beyond luck, you must go and shoot whenever you have the chance.

The photographer long wanted to shoot the windmills at Zaanse Schans, but he never got a photo that pleased him. He always went out full of hope and came back disappointed.

At last the chance came. A thick fog covered the whole windmill village that day. He arrived at this historic little village at 5 in the morning.

A photo he took for a friend made him famous at once. The friend stood under the Milky Way. To shoot this one photo, he took fifteen tries, and the final shot came at 3 in the morning.

There is only one week a year when you can shoot this, with the sun setting right in the center of a bridge arch in Amsterdam. He went to shoot five times over two years, just to get this one scene.

Landscape photography looks very simple. You just aim the camera at a pretty scene, choose the right angle and focal length, set a good exposure, and press the shutter.

A photo shot this way may look quite good, but it has one flaw. It is too ordinary.

After seeing these, you understand that a professional landscape photographer does not get great photos only from fine gear or mere luck.

It takes more effort behind the scenes, and it takes patience and passion, to shoot a photo that feels fresh.
