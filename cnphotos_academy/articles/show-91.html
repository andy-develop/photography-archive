With Google Earth, Drones Take a Bird's-Eye View of the World!

Published: 2022-09-15

Original link: https://www.cnphotos.net/academy/show-91.html

When brothers JP and Mike Andrew first shot with a drone, they were stunned by the view from the air.

The first abstract aerial art photo by the Andrew brothers

The first drone used by the Andrew brothers

Seen from the air, landscapes that look ordinary on the ground become magical and beautiful.

Inspired, the brothers began to travel the world. They decided to use their work to tell viewers how incredible this world looks from the air. That is how this set of abstract aerial art was born.

The Andrew brothers' aerial photos are marked by clever composition, and not by luck at all.

Before they arrive to shoot on site, they usually use Google Earth and other mapping software to compose the image virtually on a computer first.

In the images below, the left image is a Google Earth screenshot, and the right image is the actual shot.

Besides scouting and composing the image on Google Earth, choosing the right moment and light is also very important for the Andrew brothers.

For example, the image called Skyline shows a cargo ship sailing on the water.

The shadow that the containers on the ship cast on the water looks like a city skyline, and it catches the eye.

Skyline

'Even though some of our aerial photos look hard to believe, and people may even doubt they are real, every one of them is a real place on our planet.'

The Andrew brothers say that, apart from a slight boost to color and contrast, their photos have no other manual processing.

They hope these photos tell viewers that a whole new world on this planet is waiting to be discovered.

Whether it is cars parked in symmetry or colorful beach umbrellas, the interesting arrangements they find are a pleasure for the viewer.

'The more beauty we find, the more we feel we have only scratched the surface!'
