In still life photography, the photographer builds the image almost entirely by controlling light, mood, and composition. Because the photographer directly shapes the work, a still life photo reflects the creator's own creativity and style. For this reason, the work of still-life specialists is usually unique and creative.

The endless possibilities of still life photography can look hard, but it is worth a try. Below are 7 things to remember when you shoot still life.

1. Prepare before you shoot

When you shoot still life, you rarely get a good photo by luck. You are in charge of the whole process. The more you prepare, the better the result. Spend some time brainstorming how to carry out your idea. As you read the points below, think about how you would plan for them in advance.

Different styles of still life have their own challenges. Build those challenges, and how to beat them, into your plan. For example, jewelry photography needs a way to shoot highly reflective surfaces. You can take steps to cut reflection, such as using a light modifier to reduce the open space around the jewelry. Whatever subject you choose for your still life, keep its specific challenges in mind when you plan.

3. Composition

When you shoot still life, you can move the subject more freely. This lets you try unique compositions (without boring or frustrating the subject). Picture yourself as a graphic designer laying out the frame, and some of your work will be very interesting. You can also try different ratios between objects.

One thing to watch is the space around your subject, which is negative (or white) space. Try adding or removing negative space, and your composition may improve.

4. Lighting

Like every other kind of photography, good light is essential in still life. Because you control your surroundings almost completely, there is no excuse for poor light. In other words, work out how to perfect the light in your still-life setup.

If you rely on natural light, find the best time to shoot or wait until the light improves. When you use flash, think about diffused light, an off-camera flash, and using reflectors to guide the light.

5. Background

Make sure the background suits the main subject. For example, think about how the background looks in focus and out of focus. Try backgrounds with interesting texture, or with no texture at all. A plain background is an effective way to make the subject stand out. If you can, shoot the same subject against different types of background.

6. Take it outside

Still life does not have to stay indoors. You can find great still-life subjects outside: leaves floating in a river, a nice arrangement of street objects, or interesting rocks on a beach. It can be hard to control the setting, but outdoors you can find unique subjects and backgrounds.

7. Unleash your inner creativity

There is no limit to what you can do with still life (just know that the subject stays still). So be as creative as you like. You might be the first person to shoot a rubber duck in a bathtub full of watermelons. Some still lifes are full of meaning and symbolism, and you can do the same in your own still-life photos.
