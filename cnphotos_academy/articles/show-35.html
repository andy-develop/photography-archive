How to Improve Your Composition? 10 Very Practical Ideas!

Published: 2022-03-08

Learning composition is a required lesson for improving your photography. Below we offer a few ideas and tips for improving your composition. If you are interested, give them a try.

1. Keep the scene simple

A common mistake for new photographers is to be too greedy. They want to capture everything in front of them. Capturing every element can be a good record, but it often leaves your photo without a clear subject, so it looks too cluttered and loses the meaning of the story it tells.

Instead, try to keep the scene as simple as possible. Shoot only what is truly interesting, and remove the extra or unrelated elements. You will find that the subject stands out more.

2. Fill the frame

If there are many unrelated things or blank space around the edges, the main subject will not stand out enough. In that case, try to fill the frame. Use the zoom well, or take a few more steps, to make the photo more complete.

3. Use different photo ratios well

A horizontal frame is easier for handheld shooting, but if every photo is horizontal, it becomes dull. A vertical frame often gives a beautiful result, and it can even bring out the subject more. If you are not sure which to use, try both.

4. Do not always place the subject in the center

Many beginners always place the subject dead center, which can make the photo look plain and hard to enjoy. Try using the rule of thirds or the golden ratio well, and place the subject on one of the four intersections. Your photos will look better.

Of course, do not always place the subject exactly on a third. When you use the rule of thirds, watch the balance of the frame.

5. Use leading lines

A photo without careful composition often lacks a focus, so the reader does not know where to look. To avoid this, look for a good 'leading line' that guides the reader's eye to the subject.

A leading line does not have to be a real line. It can be a row of street lamps or a few stones. In short, find an angle that connects these separate objects. Of course, you should also make good use of real lines such as roads and railings.

6. Use diagonals

Diagonals are also a common composition method. Compared with the steady feeling of horizontal and vertical lines, diagonals are well suited to showing a sense of motion.

7. Leave room for a 'moving subject'

When people see an object, they often wonder what it will do next. So when you shoot a moving subject (whether it truly moves or only seems to), leave a little space in the direction of movement. The photo will feel more balanced.

8. Blur the background to bring out the subject

This composition method is a must for photographers. With a shallow depth of field, you can bring the subject out of a messy or complex background. It works for portraits, and also for still subjects, landscape close-ups, and architecture.

9. Make good use of color contrast

Contrast is one of the easiest and most important composition techniques. Beginners can start with color contrast. You only need to find opposite color groups to shoot, such as yellow and blue, or red and green. When you compose, you can place these two colors side by side, or use the surrounding method (for example, a single touch of red among green leaves).

10. Do not cling to theory. Work freely.

The theory of composition is only a start. When you actually shoot, you can be bold and try new things. As long as it tells the story behind the photo, a few unconventional compositions can actually make the photo more moving.

Photography values innovation, but for beginners it is still necessary to learn some basic ideas and methods of composition. The tips above are worth studying carefully.
