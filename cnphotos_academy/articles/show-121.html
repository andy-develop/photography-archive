Make the Most of Spring: 7 Ways to Capture the Season

Published: 2023-03-03

Original link: https://www.cnphotos.net/academy/show-121.html

Spring is a season people love, because the land wakes up and fills with warm signs of life. Now it is time to show the beauty of spring. For a nature photographer, spring is clearly the best subject to shoot right now.

Blooming branches

Spring is the season when flowers bloom, and cherry blossoms are its symbol. When the trees start to flower, it is a clear sign the weather is warming. A simple photo of a flower-laden branch is striking enough. You can also try bokeh or black and white. Or use the blossoms as a frame for another subject, and your photo gains a spring feel at once.

Spring flowers

Crocuses, tulips, daffodils, hyacinths. After the trees bloom, spring flowers are the next subject photographers love to shoot. Sometimes they appear before winter ends, and colorful flowers push up through the snow. It does not matter how cold the rest of the landscape looks. One spring flower sets off the whole coming of spring.

New leaves

Flowers draw the most attention in spring, but new leaves are just as lovely. Their bright green works especially well against a dark background. Like flowers, they also make a great subject for bokeh shots.

Green meadows

You can find green grass in every season now, but this fresh, bright green suits spring best. Lush green grass is also more common in spring, before the dry summer arrives. Like fresh flowers, green grass can light up a dull subject such as bare trees. Or simply shoot a close-up of dew on the grass at sunrise.

Spring birds

Birds are a subject you can shoot any time of year, but they suit spring well. They pair nicely with other spring subjects such as trees and flowers. Spring is the mating season for many birds. The birds spread colorful plumage that matches the spirit of spring, so do justice to the spring scenery.

Newborn animals

Some wildlife, such as bears and squirrels, give birth in the depths of winter. But most animals wait for spring. It is the season when survival is easiest for them. The weather warms, and food is plentiful, like the green grass mentioned earlier.

Of course, if you cannot find a newborn animal, shoot a spring-themed photo with a kitten or puppy. They turn out just as cute.

Spring festivals

People have celebrated spring for centuries, such as Easter and the Indian festival of Holi. These celebrations matter across much of the world. On top of that, the season holds smaller but still important days, such as Mother's Day. All these celebrations give a photographer a chance to capture beauty, culture, and memories.

Whatever the spring festival, it is worth a photo to mark it. Even if you cannot attend a big event or celebrate with friends or a loved one, you can still do something special for the holiday.

One tip for better spring photos

To shoot better spring photos, follow these basics:

1. Always keep your intent in mind. Do you shoot to tell a story, capture beauty, catch life, or simply record a moment? Knowing your purpose and intent makes your photos better.

2. Know the basics of color theory. To find colors that work well together, learn some color basics, such as complementary, analogous, and contrasting colors. Then find ways to pair colors so they set each other off.

3. Tell a story. Use wide, medium, and tight frames to tell the story. Start with a wide shot, move to a medium composition, and then go tighter. These three views help make the message of your photos clear.
