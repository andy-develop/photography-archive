The Ministry of Public Security Releases Typical Cases in the Lawful Crackdown on Illegal Drone Flights

Published: 2026-02-09

In recent years, the use of drones and their industrial applications have grown fast. At the same time, problems have kept appearing, such as breaking into drone information systems, illegal or rule-breaking drone flights, and even extreme illegal flights. These seriously disrupt airspace safety and even threaten civil aviation. The Ministry of Public Security takes this very seriously. Since December 2025, it has run a nationwide Clean Sky campaign to crack down hard on the crime of extreme illegal drone flights. The aim is to remove safety risks, punish illegal flights firmly, hold the bottom line of low-altitude safety, and support the high-quality growth of the low-altitude economy. To serve as a warning, the Ministry of Public Security today releases typical cases that the police have handled in recent years.

The police remind the public that the sky is not a place outside the law. Flying a drone without real-name registration, or flying a micro, light, or small drone in controlled airspace without approval, or illegally breaking into or taking control of a drone onboard information system, or providing programs or tools for that, or breaking a drone no-fly zone, flight-limited zone, or airspace-holding limits, all violate laws such as the Public Security Administration Punishments Law of the People's Republic of China, the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, and the Interim Regulations on the Flight Management of Unmanned Aircraft. These acts carry administrative penalties, and may even lead to criminal responsibility. The public should strengthen their sense of the law, resist illegal flights on their own, follow the laws and rules strictly, register in real name, operate within the rules, and fly lawfully, so that we all keep the sky safe.


1. The Li case: endangering public safety by dangerous means


In March 2024, Li paid Zhu (handled in a separate case) to break the flight-height limit on his drone. Li then flew the drone to nearly 3,000 meters, filmed video of the clouds, and posted it publicly. The police later arrested him, and he was given five days of administrative detention. Starting in January 2025, Li bought drones four times, paid others to break the height limits, flew them at extreme illegal heights, and posted the videos. Because he flew above 8,000 meters, the videos drew wide public attention. The police found that Li flew his modified drones on more than 20 extreme illegal flights. He flew above 6,000 meters several times. Some of his flight paths entered civil aviation routes, and his closest distance to a civil aircraft was about 800 meters. This seriously threatened civil aviation safety. In December 2025, Li was formally arrested on suspicion of endangering public safety by dangerous means.


2. The Tian case: endangering public safety by dangerous means


In December 2023, Tian planned to use a drone with its no-fly zones and height limits broken to fly illegally near an airport and film video, so he could raise his fame on social media. In March 2024, Tian took his drone near the airport. Even though he knew civil passenger planes were taking off or landing, he still launched the drone in a no-fly zone. He filmed video above the takeoff and landing paths of the passenger planes and posted it on social media. He also posted comments such as What is law? With both hands in my pockets, I do not know what an opponent is. Let us watch hitting a plane together, and If the plane had not flown so fast, I would have hit it. The police later arrested him. In August 2024, Tian was sentenced to three years in prison for endangering public safety by dangerous means.


3. The Chen case: endangering public safety by dangerous means


In July 2025, Chen broke the flight-height limit on his drone, flew it to more than 3,200 meters, and filmed video of the drone passing through clouds. The police later arrested him. The police found that the area where Chen flew sat between two main civil aviation routes. The lowest flight altitude for civil aircraft in that area was only about 2,800 meters. Chen act seriously affected flight safety. In January 2026, Chen was placed under criminal compulsory measures on suspicion of endangering public safety by dangerous means.


4. The Zhu case: providing programs or tools for breaking into or illegally controlling computer information systems


In 2024, Zhu paid Wu (who, in April 2023, had been sentenced to one year in prison with one year of probation for damaging computer information systems) to break the height limit on a drone, and Zhu learned the method. Starting in March of that year, Zhu began to break the height and no-fly-zone limits on other people drones for a fee, and made money from it. The police later arrested him. The police found that Zhu broke limits on more than 50 drones and made more than 40,000 yuan illegally. Some of the modified drones were used for extreme illegal flights and posed a serious threat to public safety. In December 2025, Zhu was formally arrested on suspicion of providing programs or tools for breaking into or illegally controlling computer information systems.


5. The Li case and four others: providing programs or tools for breaking into or illegally controlling computer information systems


From August 2021 to May 2023, Li broke the height and no-fly-zone limits on other people drones for a fee and made money from it. Wang and four others referred people to Li to have their drones broken and made money from it. The police later arrested all of them. The police found that Li broke limits for 221 people and made more than 90,000 yuan illegally. In December 2023, Li was sentenced to three years in prison with three years of probation and a fine for providing programs or tools for breaking into or illegally controlling computer information systems. Wang and the four others referred more than 210 people for the breaking service and made more than 40,000 yuan illegally. In December 2023, Wang and the four others were sentenced to prison terms with probation and fines for the same crime.


6. The Zhang case: providing programs or tools for breaking into or illegally controlling computer information systems


From May 2022 to February 2023, Zhang broke the height and no-fly-zone limits on other people drones for a fee and made money from it. The police later arrested him. The police found that Zhang broke limits on more than 20 drones and made more than 15,000 yuan illegally. In December 2023, Zhang was sentenced to six months in prison with one year of probation and a fine for providing programs or tools for breaking into or illegally controlling computer information systems.


7. The Wang case: forging the seal of a state organ


Since July 2025, Wang had seals made privately, stamped them on drone flight-application forms, and gave them to others. The others used the forms to ask drone makers to lift airspace limits, so they could fly illegally in controlled airspace. The police found that Wang had nine seals made privately. They were used on more than 200 flight-application forms, and Wang made more than 70,000 yuan illegally. In December 2025, Wang was placed under criminal compulsory measures on suspicion of forging the seal of a state organ.


8. The Feng case: negligently leaking state secrets


In September 2025, Feng flew a drone with a self-modified battery cell and streamed it live on social media. During the flight, it passed over a military restricted zone. The police later arrested him. The police found that the footage from Feng live flight showed the inside of a military airbase. In September 2025, Feng was placed under criminal compulsory measures on suspicion of negligently leaking state secrets.


Relevant Regulations

Article 10 of the Interim Regulations on the Flight Management of Unmanned Aircraft states that the owner of a civil unmanned aircraft must register under their real name according to the law. The detailed rules are set by the civil aviation authority of the State Council together with the relevant departments.

Article 19 of the Interim Regulations states that the state sets up controlled airspace for unmanned aircraft as needed (called controlled airspace below).

Airspace above 120 meters true altitude, airspace above air defense restricted areas and airspace restricted areas and the areas around them, military low-flying airspace, and airspace above the following areas must be set as controlled airspace:

(1) Airports and a defined area around them;

(2) The national border, the line of actual control, and the border line, on our side within a defined area;

(3) Military restricted zones, military management areas, detention sites and other classified units, and a defined area around them;

(4) Protection areas for major military-industrial facilities, control areas for nuclear facilities, production and storage areas for dangerous goods such as explosives and flammables, and large storage areas for important combustible materials;

(5) Public infrastructure such as power plants, substations, fuel and gas stations, water plants, public transport hubs, aviation and power hubs, major water facilities, ports, highways, and electrified railway lines, plus a defined area around them and drinking-water source protection zones;

(6) Facilities that need special protection of their electromagnetic environment, such as radio observatories, satellite tracking and navigation stations, aviation radio navigation stations, and radar stations, plus a defined area around them;

(7) Major revolutionary memorial sites, important immovable cultural relics, and a defined area around them;

(8) Other areas set by the national air traffic control authority.

The exact scope of controlled airspace is set by air traffic control bodies at each level under the rules of the national air traffic control authority. It is announced by the people government at or above the level of a city with districts. The civil aviation authority and the units that carry the related duties release the aeronautical information.

Without approval from an air traffic control body, no one may fly an unmanned aircraft in controlled airspace.

Airspace outside controlled areas is suitable flying airspace for micro, light, and small unmanned aircraft.
