What Comes to Mind to Keep the Beauty of Summer?

Published: 2023-07-27

Source link: https://www.cnphotos.net/academy/show-147.html

Every season offers a chance to take beautiful photos, but summer offers the most. A sunny day at the beach, children playing in a pool or a fountain, fireflies glowing through the long summer nights. These are not only beautiful moments and memories. They are also a good time to photograph life.

Summer is full of fun events and activities, but if you do not know how to capture the moment, you may regret missing it. If you lack inspiration, here are some summer photo ideas, so you can shoot and enjoy the hottest time of the year.

The beach

A beach is beautiful in every season, but a summer beach is busier. That extra activity means more subjects, from sunbathers and surfers to sea views and sunsets. You can even try underwater photography.

If you do not live by the sea, try a sandy beach by a lake. Depending on the size and clarity of the lake, you can get similar photos, just on a smaller scale.

The swimming pool

No beach nearby? Try a local swimming pool. If your home is large enough, you can also buy an inflatable pool for your own yard.

A pool may not offer glamorous nature, but it lets you easily shoot swimmers and children playing in the water. You do not deal with waves, and you do not need a telephoto for close-ups. Even a simple point-and-shoot camera works well at a pool.

Fountains

Fountains are usually a summer hotspot, drawing tourists, families, and others who want to cool off. Whether a fountain is large or small, a grand landmark or a small one for children, it offers many subjects. Besides candid portraits, you can shoot sunlight glinting through the water or the buildings around the fountain. The splashing water is also a good chance to practice long exposure or fast action.

Sprinklers

Without a fountain, you can use a small sprinkler to create a similar playful mood. A sprinkler may not be as beautiful as a fountain, but it is easier to find, buy, and move. You can place it where you want, for the right background or composition.

Sailboats

Summer is the peak season for sailing. The warm weather, good light, and relaxed mood suit sailboats and the shooting of them.

You can take good photos along the coast, but for a unique angle and a more memorable experience, try sailing out yourself. Then you can shoot your own boat and the sailboats around you.

Blue sky

Water is a big part of summer, but not every summer photo has to involve water. You can also capture the summer mood with a sunny blue sky. All you need is one element that suggests summer, such as a person lying down or a lush green field, and the photo feels like summer.

When you shoot a blue sky, keep a graduated neutral-density filter or a polarizing filter handy. These filters prevent overexposure and make the deep blue more vivid.

Also, try to include some clouds. A blue sky is beautiful, but without clouds for accent and contrast it feels flat. If the sky has no clouds, delay the shoot. In the long summer days, you have plenty of time for the right photo.

Fireflies

If you are lucky enough to live where there are fireflies, make the most of it. Shooting fireflies takes some practice, but once you master it, the images have an unbelievable, surreal natural beauty.

Shooting fireflies is like shooting star trails, and it needs long exposure, so you need a sturdy tripod and a remote shutter. But fireflies are trickier than stars, because they flash briefly rather than glow steadily. This fast flash is hard to balance with the background light, which builds up during the exposure.

To balance the two light sources, use a high ISO and a short exposure. Start at 5 seconds, and adjust. Then, in post, you can stack the photos to build one image full of fireflies. This limits noise while keeping the fireflies bright and beautiful.
