Glass Windows and Rain: 8 Ways to Shoot the Mood and Atmosphere of a Rainy Day

Published: 2023-04-10

Source link: https://www.cnphotos.net/academy/show-125.html

A rainy day is perfect for sitting on the sofa under a blanket with a hot drink. If you are a photographer, you can also use your camera. Even without going out, you can take special rain photos through the window. Today we explore 8 creative ways to shoot rainy windows.

Try bokeh

Raindrops on a window can create a lovely bokeh under the right light. In general, the best bokeh comes from shooting bright lights through a rain-streaked window in a dull or dark setting. For example, shooting car headlights at night through a car window works very well.

You can also shoot pretty bokeh of sunlight and rain. This can be harder, because the sun needs to hit the raindrops at an angle.

Bold color composition

Rainy days are usually gray, which makes the image dull, especially the view through a window. But if you shoot something bright on the other side of the glass, this gray can help. The dull gray sets off the color, and the contrast makes it pop. Even if the rainy window softens the subject a little, the bright color is enough to make the image interesting.

Switch to black and white

Black and white is another way to make a dull scene striking, because it draws attention to the contrast of light. This emphasis on light makes the subject look clearer and brings out patterns that color hides. Black and white also suits the mood of a rainy day, which is dim and bleak.

Choose a recognizable subject

Whether you shoot in color or black and white, you are more likely to get a beautiful photo if the subject is easy to recognize. If it is not, the viewer may be puzzled by the photo and why you took it, especially when the subject is blurred or half-hidden by rain. If your subject is something recognizable, such as a tree or a building, the viewer will still read your photo well even through the blur.

Shoot portraits

People are among the easiest subjects to recognize. Even when rain covers them, they usually draw the eye, especially when you show their eyes. One facial expression can tell a whole story at once and add emotional weight that makes the photo more compelling.

Focus on the raindrops

When you cannot find a good subject or angle through the window, try focusing on the raindrops themselves. Look around the window for interesting patterns, then move in close. If you are lucky, the drops will bend the background upside down. Even if they do not, their shape and color alone are enough for a beautiful photo.

Objects by the window

If you cannot find a good subject through the window, place a striking subject beside it. A colored object such as a red leaf is usually a good choice, and so is a person. Even a handprint can make an ordinary window look interesting. Try different ideas and compositions. It is a great way to practice and improve your photography on a rainy day.

Create your own rain

If you live where it rarely rains, you may think this subject is not for you. But even if you do not live in a rainy place, you can experiment yourself. Below is an example of a shot taken when it was not actually raining. Just spray water on a sheet of acrylic and hold it between your subject and the camera. Try lighting it from different angles for interesting results.

Finally

Photos of rainy windows always have something special. Maybe they stir nostalgia, or give you a glimpse of a world you do not usually see, or catch a moment of calm in a busy world. In a world full of noise and distraction, a photo of a rainy window offers a short rest. It is a chance to step back and enjoy the simple beauty around us. Whether it is the subtle glint of raindrops on the glass or the soft light of a rainy day, these photos remind us of the beauty of the simplest things.
