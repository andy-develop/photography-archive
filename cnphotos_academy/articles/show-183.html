Promoting Culture-Tourism Integration and Capturing Beautiful Views: Leading Photographers Focus on the Maling Mountain Scenic Area

Published: 2024-07-19

Text by Song Yunda

“Peaks rise in a thousand folds, far finer than the fairy isles.” This is a verse left by the Qing dynasty scholar Ji Xiaolan when he accompanied Emperor Qianlong to Maling Mountain.

Today Maling Mountain links five peaks, embraces seven lakes, and spreads 68 scenic spots among green hills and waters, forming a poetic landscape scroll that draws visitors from all sides. Recently a group of special guests came here, carrying long and short lenses and drones, and captured the graceful views of Maling Mountain from their own angles.

Spiritual hills and clear waters, green as spring all year. Faced with such beauty, Liu Jun, former vice chairman of the Chinese Photographers Association and an outstanding photographer of its first De-Yi-Shuang-Xin award, quickly chose a good spot, flew the drone himself, and shot these bright landscape scrolls from the air.

The Maling Ancient Road is one of the core sights of Maling Mountain. This was the ancient battlefield of the Battle of Maling, where Sun Bin and Pang Juan fought their legendary duel of wits. Today, a large sculptural group of the Battle of Maling lies hidden in the valley woods, and visitors feel as if they have stepped into the battle of old. No wonder Song Yuangao, a famous landscape photographer and army veteran who is a member of the Chinese Photographers Association, and Chang Zechun, secretary-general of the drone aerial committee of the Chinese Art Photography Association,

stopped there and pressed the shutter again and again.

Three Immortals Lake and Three Immortals Cave are traditional sights of the Maling Mountain area. This is where Pu Songling once gathered folk material and wrote the Three Immortals tale that became famous. More legend still: this Three Immortals Cave, home to the toad immortal, the crab immortal, and the snake immortal, later became the forward command post of Chen Yi and Su Yu in the northern Jiangsu campaign, and the lake and cave grew famous. Deng Wei, former vice chairman of the Chinese Photographers Association, Dai Tianrong, a director of the association and winner of the China Photography Gold Statue Award, and the well-known travel photographers Ji Guilin and Cheng Xuefeng came here and captured the lake and cave scenes.

The Great Dragon Gully is a new spot opened on a two-kilometer lakeside valley, based on the legend of the dragon's nine sons. The gully is deep and wooded, with hill and lake together. Not only does the Great Dragon Falls pour down, but the Nine Dragon Sons pavilions are built on the gently winding water, and each pavilion tells a story of a dragon son, which draws the visitor in. Jia Mingzu, former vice chairman of the Chinese Photographers Association, along with Ji Guilin and Chang Zechun, captured the beauty here.

The Little Dragon Gully has flying falls and flowing springs, birdsong and flower scent, with cicadas and frogs. It is a green spot built fully on natural ecology. Forest cover is over 97%, and the summer average is 4 to 5 degrees cooler than the city. It is a good place to relax and escape the heat, and a destination for young people to visit and meet. Zhang Lei, winner of the China Photography Gold Statue Award, and Yuan Dongping, editor of the Nationality Pictorial and a photography educator, opened their lenses here.

The Huating Tribe site is the place with the deepest culture in the area. The 5,000-year-old Huating cultural site unearthed here is the only “two-in-one” culture in China that combines features of both Liangzhu and Dawenkou culture. It is recognized by archaeologists at home and abroad as one of the ‌horizons of human civilization,” the “eastern earthen pyramid.” The photographers came here and left deep memories with their lenses.

The beauty of Maling lies in Maling itself. Hold fast to the green hills, and make the creation dream come true. Now the Maling Mountain Scenic Area uses the push to build a national 5A-level tourist attraction as its drive. It digs deep into the history and culture of Maling Mountain, raises the quality of its tourism products, and keeps growing the area's appeal and market influence. The future of Maling Mountain is bright.
