World-Famous Photographers: How Do You Capture Humor in Street Photography?

Published: 2022-02-17

In a time when everyone takes photos, street photography is a category that is easy to enter and pick up. Many amateurs treat snapshots as street photography, but that is not really the case. Street photography has produced many masters in the history of photography, from the documentary side and the everyday-life side, and also the conceptual side.

Among the kinds of street photography, the one that attracts people most is fun photography. This kind uses visual misdirection and contrast to seize clever and rare moments, showing coincidence, humor, and absurdity. Many works do more than make you laugh. They also make you think, so they keep their seriousness and social meaning.

Today I introduce three good street photographers: the French Magnum photographer René Maltête, Elliott Erwitt, and the British photographer Matt Stuart.

Their work draws directly from moments in life, and it shows close observation and casual humor everywhere. You can feel their wit and optimism in the photos. What is valuable is that the work is light and happy but not shallow, and humorous yet still serious. I hope you can learn something from it.

1. René Maltête

René Maltête (1930-2000) was an overlooked French photographer and poet. He started photography at 16, and liked to shoot fun things with visual misdirection. His humorous photos focus on the mismatch of time and space. They surprise people, yet they often carry a gentle philosophical thought.

2. Elliott Erwitt

Elliott Erwitt was born in Paris, France, in 1928. In 1953, at only 25, he joined Magnum Photos under Cartier-Bresson. His work draws from everyday life, and it is known for its gentle irony.

3. Matt Stuart

Matt Stuart once played trumpet in a symphony orchestra. Under his father's influence he took up photography. Inspired by Henri Cartier-Bresson and Robert Frank, he devoted himself fully to street photography. He has shot for 18 years, and he treats street photography as his way of understanding other people.
