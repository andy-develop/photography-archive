The Rarest 100 Years of Chinese New Year Photos: the World Changes, the Festival Fire Remains

Published: 2022-02-07

For Chinese people, the Spring Festival is still a most important ritual. The truest feeling between people lives in those scenes full of daily life.

Here are dozens of old photos, showing how Chinese people spent the Spring Festival over the last 100 years.

Below is a set of photos of Shanghai people's Spring Festival, taken by Americans.

During the Spring Festival, the streets and lanes are lively.

The photo below shows a busy Shanghai street.

Everyone is hurrying back to reunite with family and celebrate the New Year.

A man spins a diablo. Onlookers of all ages watch closely. The two children on the sides watch especially hard, afraid to miss a move. The newsstand owner at the right rear looks on with a hint of disdain.

The original meaning of putting up couplets at Spring Festival was to drive away evil. Now it also adds festive color. It is a must for nearly every household. This old photo shows a couplet vendor writing the top scroll: no taboos.

Setting off firecrackers is the first Spring Festival custom, marking the leaving of the old and the welcome of the new. It is also a favorite of children. This old photo shows a man lighting crackers, while the smaller children hide behind him, waiting for the bang.

Besides new clothes and dumplings, lucky money is what children most look forward to. Before the New Year dinner, juniors pay respects to elders, and elders give red envelopes, urging the juniors to do better in study in the new year. After the meal, the elders hand out the money and urge the children to learn and grow. This old photo shows a mother giving lucky money to her child. The older brother, who got his money first, stands straight and waits for the photo.

In front of the house, parents hold a child and share the New Year joy with neighbors. The child's hands are open, waiting for someone to hold them.

A smiling man holds a money-leaf, a wish for wealth in the year to come.

During the Spring Festival, no matter where you are, you return home to share the festival with family. This photo shows a grandparent and grandchild playing in front of the house. The baby just learned to walk and watches the crowd nearby, while the grandma holds on tight.
