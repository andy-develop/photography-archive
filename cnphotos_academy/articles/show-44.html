Master This Technique and You Will Get Great Shots

Published: 2022-03-18

Photography is essentially organization. It means putting the image elements that enter the frame in order. The most important way to bring order is contrast.

Contrast stresses the difference between image elements in a photo, whether in tone, color, form, or other. Two contrasting elements strengthen each other and bring out their own force. In photography, look more, think more, and shoot more. Below we enjoy how masters use contrast.

1. Black and white

In life, high-contrast work is easy to remember. When you frame with the camera, if you can spot the traits of things and use contrast to compose, you make the photo easier to read.

Photo: Vladislav Kobets

2. Main and secondary

The position of objects in the frame brings out the subject. In composition, a thing in the center stands out more than one at the edge.

3. Hidden and revealed

Hidden means an incomplete form, and revealed means a complete form. The balance of hidden and revealed makes a photo subtle and leaves room to savor.

4. Line contrast

Lines, in their horizontal or vertical, long or short, curved or straight, and thick or thin forms, make different visual shapes and stir different feelings. As one of the basic elements of composition, the photographer should use the changes in lines to contrast and express his artistic idea.

5. High and low

In a single frame, it is often hard to show the true height of an object. The photo below uses a low-angle shot. It lowers the horizon and pushes the distant object down to create a different visual shape.

6. Blurred and sharp

Blurred and sharp is a common technique. The blurred supports the sharp. Below, the blurred shadow is used well to contrast with the sharp building, and the viewer stays longer on the work.

7.  Sparse and dense

Dense enough to block wind, sparse enough to ride a horse is the old saying about the relation between sparse and dense. It also fits photographic composition. Use the number of elements in an area to set off two or more areas. Even if one spot is small, it can become the center of attention.

8. Cool and warm

Cool and warm refer to temperature. Red, pink, and orange feel warm, while purple and cyan feel cold.

9. Moving and still

What was a still, beautiful landscape becomes lively at once when a moving thing enters the frame.

10. Light and dark

In a photo, the tones of black, gray, and white, in their strong and weak forms, set the base of every picture. They are a key means for the photographer to finish the composition and express an idea. Different tones give different visual feelings. The photo below meters the main subject and then controls the exposure based on a high, middle, or low tone.

11. Large and small

If you want to show the grandeur of a scene, you must first know that without the small there is no large. In a photo, without a reference, the viewer cannot feel how vast or striking the scene is.

12. Hard and soft

Hard and soft is often used with women. The hard sets off the softness of a woman. The cold, solid wall makes the figure in the center look frail and helpless.

13. New and old

Photography can use the contrast of new and old to show change and growth. For example, compare the new look of a city with its old face. The image above uses a run-down old house in the foreground to contrast sharply with the tall buildings in the distance, and the viewer feels the fast change of the city. You can also compare several photos taken at different times.

14. Bustling and desolate

Strong contrast leaves you wanting more and stirs the imagination. It seems as if another story is about to be told.

Life holds many more such pairs, such as modern and backward, left and right. In photography they exist as opposites. From the view of composition, they complete each other, and neither can be missing. Do you see the contrast in the works below?

In short, whether these pairs are high or low in saturation, they are only a form of expression, and they serve your message in the end.

A great photo does not become great because it copies some great style. It becomes great because the photographer uses his craft to reach at least two of these goals:

a. It restores the truth.

b. It shows beauty.

c. It conveys emotion.

d. It presents an idea.

So first discover the traits of things, then try to compose with contrast.
