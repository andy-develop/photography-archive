How to Shoot Better Snow Photos: The Tips Are Here!

Published: 2022-02-08

For a photographer, winter is not complete without snow scenes.I have seen many snow photos on social feeds, and they are too plain. Today let us look at how the masters shoot them.

These four works below approach the snow scene from framing, detail and texture, light, and color. They show us a different kind of snow photo.

In your own shooting, you can start from these points to make a snow photo.

  1. Foreground  

Foreground framing is common in landscape shots. Branches, snowflakes, stones, and rivers can all be a foreground. Note this: when you shoot a scene with a foreground, the background must be clean.


photo by Ole Henrik Skjelstad

You can also use the reflection in a car's side mirror to create an offset foreground.

photo by Callum Snape

In the cold winter, bring a lamp. It acts as a foreground for framing, and it adds mood to the photo.

photo by Eric Bunting

  2. Lines  

Using lines in framing shows the tension of the image better. A road and a car together is the most common.

photo by Ben Prescott

You can also shoot from below to show the lines of the trees.

photo by Konsta Punkka

  3. Color  

In a winter that is all white, you can use a person's clothing to show the contrast of color.

photo by Dalkom

A string of LED lights like this adds mood to your frame.

photo by Dylan Kato


photo by Daniel Rericha

  4. Light  

No matter the season, if you use light well, the texture of the photo stays good. When you shoot snow, try backlight and side-backlight often.

photo by Isac Svanberg

photo by Thomas Waerness

Find a dark background to show the texture of the snowflakes.

photo by Joni Niemela 

Show the soft beauty of the image.

phot by Daniel Rericha

  5. Beauty of detail  

You can use a close-up to show the detail of anything.

photo by Scott Kranz

photo by Keith Ladzinski

  6. Try different lenses  

A telephoto lens has its own beauty.

photo by Callum Snape

photo by Callum Snape

A wide-angle lens has its own beauty.

photo by Dag Ole Nordhaug

An aerial shot shows the snow scene on a larger scale.

  7. Slow shutter  

For outdoor photographers, the ND8 and ND1000 are two essential filters.

The ND1000 cuts exposure by 10 stops. Use the ND1000 in daylight or when the light is strong.

photo by Arild Heitmann

The ND8 cuts exposure by about three stops. You usually use it when the light is weak. Most polarizing filters now also cut three stops, so if you buy a polarizer, you do not need the ND8.

photo by Konsta Punkka

To shoot a misty snow scene, expose for about 60 seconds. Besides making sure your tripod is steady, use a remote shutter release.

photo by Lucho J Torres
