6 Techniques and 30 Examples to Teach You Silhouette Composition

Published: 2023-04-12

Source link: https://www.cnphotos.net/academy/show-126.html

Silhouette photography is a timeless subject. It is easy for beginners to shoot, and it is also a lot of fun.

You can shoot a silhouette indoors or outdoors. Just put the subject backlit against the sky, or use more complex techniques such as off-camera flash or post processing. You can also shoot a dark subject such as a crow against a light background, and convert it to black-and-white to create a simulated silhouette.

▲ Prisoner of the Past (c) Matt Nelson

Whatever method you choose, you must think carefully about composition, so the outline is clear and striking.

The basics of silhouette composition

Before we go into techniques and inspiration, you first need a good outline for the methods we discuss later to work. The best silhouettes are usually recognizable. The viewer should tell what the subject is at a glance. If the subject is unclear, the viewer may be confused and less able to enjoy your image.

Objects that read easily as silhouettes include people, trees, mountains, bridges, and some animals such as birds or camels. Distinctive things such as a bicycle or a camera tripod also make great outlines.

▲ Two... (c) Gerardo "Tito" Paez

Once you find a subject worth shooting, keep the following composition tips in mind:

Keep it simple.

The more you put into the image, the more the silhouette has to fight for attention. This holds for any subject, but it matters especially for silhouettes because they are dark. If your image includes unnecessary colorful elements, the bold colors will steal the show.

This does not mean you should avoid all color, but color should work with the outline as a background or a leading line. If there is too much color or your image is too complex, the outline loses its power. Stick to simplicity, and you will get better images.

Frame your silhouette subject

Where it is hard or impossible to keep things simple, you need other composition tips to make the silhouette outline stand out. These tips help create order centered on the outline.

Using a frame is a great way to create order from chaos. Put the silhouette inside a frame, and it will directly draw the viewer's eye. The frame can be a quadrilateral, such as a door or a window, or a loose two-sided frame, such as a path lined with trees, which also works well.

Use diagonals and horizontal lines

Another way to draw attention to a silhouette is to place it on a horizontal or diagonal line, or make the silhouette itself into a line. Either way, a line is a strong compositional tool. A strong line can make an everyday subject look very striking.

If you shoot a horizontal line, keep it as straight as you can. A crooked horizon can ruin the whole image. You can straighten a line in post processing, but it is best to set it straight when you shoot. On some cameras, including smartphones, you can use a grid in the viewfinder, so you do not have to guess to get a level horizon.

Try symmetrical patterns

In silhouettes, you can make the image symmetrical in two ways. First, you can find a symmetrical pattern in the background, as in the examples below. Second, you can make the outline itself symmetrical. In both cases, symmetry makes your image look calm and harmonious. It is also easy to try, because many outlines can be symmetrical, such as windows, doors, people, and bridges.

Repeated patterns

Like symmetry, a repeated pattern can give your image a sense of balance and harmony. Unlike symmetry, a repeated pattern does not have to be perfect. In fact, a break in the pattern can add interest to some images. A row of unusual windows can draw attention and give the viewer something interesting to imagine.

Tell a story with your silhouette

A beautiful silhouette is not the only thing worth shooting. In fact, some outlines are powerful because they tell a story. This is especially true when you shoot a person's silhouette. Even if the silhouette is beautiful, it is the story behind it that finally holds the viewer's attention.
