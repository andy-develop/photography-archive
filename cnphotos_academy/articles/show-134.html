Not enough style? Add black and white? Wrong!

As phone cameras get better, more people take photos with their phones.

Do you know you can take black-and-white photos with your phone?

Most people shoot in color. Try black and white, and you may spot a different scene.

You think an amateur throwaway shot becomes moody just by turning it black and white? Wrong!

Today let us learn tips for black-and-white photos, so you take ones that really look good.

1、The black-and-white filter

Phone cameras are set to color by default.

But for the best result, try black and white.

Why?

Some elements look better in black and white, such as light, shadow, patterns, shapes, and lines.

Removing color lets you see these elements clearly, and shapes, lines, and patterns stand out.

So how do you take black-and-white photos with a phone?

Many people shoot in color and convert later.

I suggest shooting in black and white directly.

It is simple. Open the camera, find Filter, and pick the black-and-white filter. To return to color, pick the Original filter.

2、Shoot minimal black-and-white street photos

The best photos are often the simplest!

The fewer elements in the frame, the more they stand out.

This is especially true for black-and-white street photography.

So if you want great black-and-white street shots, aim for minimal composition.

How?

Include plenty of empty negative space.

But how do you find empty negative space in a city?

It sounds hard, but it is easier than you think.

The best way is to find an open spot in the city.

Squares, parks, and cultural sites are great for minimal street photography.

Then wait for a person to enter the scene. In composition, leave plenty of space around them.

Negative space lets you control attention, and pulls the eye to the subject.

Also think about where the subject sits in the frame.

I usually place the subject in the center. Try a few positions anyway.

Finally, avoid any visual clutter.

Move or shoot from another angle to cut unwanted elements.

A low angle adds more sky and eases the foreground.

3、Use leading lines

As photographers, our key goal is to control the viewer attention.

Leading lines are the best way.

Leading lines literally guide the eye to the subject.

Cities are full of them.

Roads, paths, bridges, walls, fences, signs, tunnels, underpasses, and other architecture can all act as leading lines.

Start where you stand and let lines run from foreground into the distance.

Ideally the subject sits where the lines meet far away.

Lines starting at a corner give the strongest impact.

This adds structure and completeness, pulling the eye from the foreground.

If the lines are not placed well, do not worry. You can crop later to perfect the composition.

Most city lines are straight. Do not forget curved ones, such as spiral stairs.

Climb to the top and shoot down to catch the spiral. It looks striking.

If a person walks along the line, or even at the bottom of the stairs, that is great!

But that takes some luck.

The spiral stairs look great on their own, so take a few shots even with no person.

4、Find patterns in the city

Patterns are another way to make black-and-white street shots interesting.

I love patterns!

Repeated patterns catch the eye at once. When viewers look at the detail, they are drawn into the frame.

In cities, modern buildings offer many patterns.

Many modern facades have repeated shapes or lines. Even a row of windows, columns, or arches can form a pattern.

When you find a repeated pattern, think about the angle.

I usually shoot straight at the building for symmetry.

A side angle works too, as the photos below show.

Once composed, wait for a person to enter.

Patterns make a stunning background, and the person becomes a strong focal point.

5、Frame the subject with architecture

Framing the subject with architecture grabs attention.

This works well in black-and-white street shots. With color removed, the strong shape of the frame stands out.

Cities have many architectural frames: arches, tunnels, doorways, and windows are great.

Once you find a good frame, wait for a person to enter.

As the person crosses, use burst mode and pick the shot with the best position and pose.

I like symmetry with the person centered, but placing them at the edge also works.

If you shoot from inside looking out, you may need to adjust exposure (image brightness).

Usually reduce exposure so bright areas keep detail.

In the phone camera app, set focus by tapping, then slide up or down to adjust exposure.

When you reduce exposure, shadows darken, even to pure black.

You lose some shadow detail, but it looks great.

6、Shoot silhouette street photos

Have you tried silhouettes with your phone?

Silhouettes suit creative street shots and look great in black and white.

The dark outline grabs attention, and the lack of detail adds mystery.

So how do you shoot a silhouette?

First, shoot toward the light, with the subject in front of a bright background.

The golden hour after sunrise or before sunset is ideal. When the sun is near the horizon, you easily get bright light behind the subject.

A bright sky at other times works too.

Try other light sources, such as street lamps, lit shop windows, or bright walls.

If you shoot at the sky, use a lower angle so the sky behind the subject is brighter.

Then reduce exposure to darken the image.

Remember, the goal is a black outline of the subject against a brighter background.

7、Shoot unique shadows

Want more unique black-and-white street shots?

Try shooting shadows.

Not many people try this, so it is interesting when done well.

Shadows add mystery and drama.

Shadows are hard to see on overcast days, so shoot in sunlight.

The best time for shadows is the golden hour, after sunrise or before sunset.

When the sun sits on the horizon, you get long shadows.

Like silhouettes, reduce exposure to darken the shadow.

Do not worry about losing shadow detail. It looks great.

When shooting shadows, recall the basic elements of black-and-white street work: leading lines, patterns, frames, and so on.

The only difference is you shoot the subject shadow, not the person.

It is also a great chance to shoot yourself, or rather your shadow.

8、Edit your street photos

Shooting is only the start. Some post work refines the photo.

You can use pro editing software such as Photoshop、Lightroom .

Or use the phone editing app, which suits beginners.

Simple tweaks to brightness, contrast, shadows, and highlights change the final image a lot.

For black-and-white images, raising contrast (or adjusting shadows and highlights) is a good start.

Cropping is another simple, powerful fix.

Why? Because perfect composition in camera is hard.

Maybe you framed too wide. Cropping highlights the subject.

Or you missed distracting elements at the edge. Just crop them out.

The horizon may look slightly tilted, or the leading lines could not start at a corner.

Cropping fixes all of these.

If clutter cannot be cropped out, use dedicated post tools to fix it.

Remember, the best photos are often the simplest.

Remove distracting objects, and keep the frame as clean as possible.
