This Swiss Woman Photographer Really Knows How to Use Light

Published: 2022-02-09

The female body has long been one of the favorite subjects of artists.

The reason is simple, in one word: beauty.

But too often, this beauty has become a kind of original sin. It is treated as a product, objectified, and consumed.

Yet in the intimate photography of the Swiss photographer Chantal Convertini, we see the most beautiful and fitting portrayal of the female body.

Her portrayal has no condescending gaze. It is a private conversation with the woman, warm and quiet. Most of all, it gives the female body back to the woman herself.

Though we often do not see the model's face or read her expression, this does not weaken the expression of emotion at all.

Precisely because she often shoots a woman's back or a part of her body, the subtle emotions hidden deep inside are amplified. At the same time, a restrained sense of space is kept, which gives the frame more texture and makes the result linger in the mind.

In this play of light and shadow, when light falls on the human body, the light gains a soul and the body takes on a divine quality.

Light lets us feel time, temperature, and mood.

In this moment, all is silent, yet the silence speaks louder than words. It feels as if we are not looking at plain photos, but at a touching frame from a textured film.

She really knows how to use light.

Whether natural light or artificial light, she uses it with real skill. She catches the subtle moment when light falls on the body, so the female body looks as pure as an ancient Greek sculpture.

Let us look at how she uses light.

Chantal Convertini fell in love with photography from the age of 15, when she got her first camera.

People, nature, the city. She carried her camera through streets and alleys and used film to hold different views.

Like most young girls, she also liked taking selfies.

In the lens of the self, her body no longer needed the judgment of others. It became a full expression of self-recognition.

Which pose is most beautiful, which angle is most charming. A woman should know and accept her own body better than anyone, instead of waiting for others to discover and approve of it.

Beyond selfies, Chantal Convertini extended this expression into other work. She helped many girls find the key to their own bodies, and show the grace of their bodies and their unique selves.

Photo after photo, full of emotion and story, came to life. They are not works made for others to view. They are a woman's own keepsake, recording the time she spends alone.
