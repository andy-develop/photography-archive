I Looked at These Snow Compositions 10 Times. Do You See the Photographer's Eye?

Published: 2025-12-06

For a photographer, you must not miss a snowy scene in winter.I have seen many snow photos on social media, and most are too ordinary. Today let us look at how the masters shoot them.

In the four works below, the photographers worked through composition, texture of detail, light and shadow, and color. They show us snow photos that are different.

In your daily shooting, you can also start from these points to make a snow photo.

1. Foreground

Foreground composition is used often in landscape work. Branches, snowflakes, stones, and rivers can all serve as a foreground. But note that when you shoot with a foreground, the background must be clean.



photo by Ole Henrik Skjelstad

You can also use the reflection in a car's rearview mirror to create a sense of a shifted foreground.

photo by Callum Snape

On a cold winter day, set up a lamp. It works both as a foreground in the composition and as a way to add mood to the photo.

photo by Eric Bunting

2. Lines

Composing with lines expresses the tension of the frame better. The combination of a road and a car is the most common.

photo by Ben Prescott

You can also shoot from a low angle to show the linear feel of trees.

photo by Konsta Punkka

3. Color

In winter, when the ground is all white, you can use a person's clothing to show the contrast of color.

photo by Dalkom

LED string lights like these add mood to your frame.

photo by Dylan Kato


photo by Daniel Rericha

4. Light

No matter the season, if you use the light well, the texture of the photo will not disappoint. When you shoot snow, try backlight and side backlight often.

photo by Isac Svanberg

photo by Thomas Waerness

Find a dark background to show the grain of the snowflakes.

photo by Joni Niemela 

Show the hazy beauty of the frame.

phot by Daniel Rericha

5. The beauty of detail

You can use a close-up to show the beauty of detail in any subject.

photo by Scott Kranz

photo by Keith Ladzinski

6. Try different lenses

A telephoto lens has its own beauty.

photo by Callum Snape

photo by Callum Snape

A wide-angle lens has its own beauty.

Aerial shooting shows the snow on a larger scale.

photo by Konsta Punkka

7. Slow-shutter shooting

For outdoor photographers, the ND8 and ND1000 are two essential filters.

An ND1000 reduces exposure by 10 stops. Use it in daylight or when the light is strong.

photo by Arild Heitmann

An ND8 reduces exposure by 3 stops and is usually used when the light is weak. Most polarizers today also reduce exposure by 3 stops, so if you buy a polarizer you do not need an ND8.

photo by Konsta Punkka

To shoot a misty snow scene, the exposure is usually around 60 seconds. Besides making sure your tripod is steady, it is best to use a shutter release.

photo by Lucho J Torres

So, are the masters' snow photos striking? You can learn these methods too.
