Announcement: Winning Works of the 5th Bird's-Eye Vision National Aerial Photography Contest 2024 - Humanistic Documentary Category

Published: 2025-12-05 | Category: Humanistic Documentary

Hosted by China Photo Network and co-organized by People's Photography, the Entrepreneurs Photography Association (Shenzhen), the Henan Aerial Photography Association, the Hebei Aerial Photography Society, the Anhui Aerial Flight Corps, and the cultural-tourism photography associations of Tianjin, Zhejiang, Liaoning, Jiangxi, Shaanxi, Fujian, Hubei, and Hebei, plus the HK-SZ-Zh-Macau photo society and the Hangzhou aerial branch, the 5th Bird's-Eye Vision National Aerial Photography Contest 2024 ran a four-month call. It received 73,267 still works (series) and 511 short videos from 8,191 photographers across 31 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities, Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan, and countries such as Russia, Singapore, Australia, Malaysia, the United States, and New Zealand.
After the call closed, entries were pre-judged at a selection rate of about 10 percent. In the end, 7,310 still works (series) and 109 short videos moved to the final round. The final judging used a selection rate of about 1.2 percent, as in past years. It chose 1,095 still works (series) and 65 short videos for tier and excellence awards across five categories: Urban Landscape, Nature, Humanistic Documentary, Abstract Art, and Short Video. The proposed winners and the list were posted for one week, from December 5 to 12, 2025, on all China Photo Network platforms.

During the public notice, the public and photographers took an active part in oversight, which protected the fairness of the contest. The jury took the reported and flagged cases very seriously, and checked and reviewed each one.

After strict checks, five works were disqualified. The problems fell into three groups. First, the submitted works had no EXIF data, and the authors could not provide the original files when asked, which fails the authenticity standard. Second, the works were actually shot with a DSLR, without the bird's-eye view of aerial work, and the authors could not prove aircraft assistance, which fails the core aerial requirement. Third, the shooting height clearly exceeded the flight limits, and the authors could not provide valid flight-report data, which suggests a flight violation and fails the safety rule.

The organizing committee has always held to fairness, justice, and openness, and takes the rules and the authenticity checks seriously. Removing these works protects the credibility of the contest and the rights of the entrants, and keeps the professional quality of the event. We thank the public for their oversight and support. We will keep working on the winning works, and present a high-quality aerial art show.
