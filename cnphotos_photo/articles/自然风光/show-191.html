Announced: Winning Works of the 5th Bird's-Eye View National Aerial Photography Competition, 2024 — Nature Category

Published: 2025-12-05 | Category: Nature | Source:https://www.cnphotos.net/photo/show-191.html

The 5th Bird's-Eye View National Aerial Photography Competition, 2024, was hosted by China Photo Network and co-organized by the People's Photography newspaper, the Entrepreneurs Photography Association, Shenzhen, aerial photography associations across the provinces, cultural and tourism photography associations, the Hong Kong-Shenzhen-Zhuhai-Macao photography society, and the aerial branch of the Hangzhou Photographers Association. After more than four months of open submission, it received 73,267 photo series and 511 short videos from 8,191 photographers. They came from 31 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities, from Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan, and from countries such as Russia, Singapore, Australia, Malaysia, the United States, and New Zealand.
After the deadline, the entries were given a first review at an acceptance rate of about 10 percent. In the end, 7,310 photo series and 109 short videos moved to the final review. The final review used the usual acceptance rate of about 1.2 percent. It selected 1,095 photo series and 65 short videos for graded and honorable awards across five categories: urban landscape, nature, humanistic documentary, abstract art, and short video. The proposed winners were posted for a week, from December 5 to December 12, 2025, on all China Photo Network platforms.

During the public notice period, people across society and many photographers took part in oversight, which was key to a fair and just competition. The jury took the reported violations of individual works seriously and checked and reviewed each case one by one.

After a strict check, the award status of 5 works was withdrawn. The violations fell mainly into three groups. First, some submitted works had no EXIF data, and the authors could not provide the original file when asked during the raw-file check, so they did not meet the authenticity standard. Second, some works were actually shot with a DSLR and lacked the bird's-eye view that aerial work should have, and the authors could not provide valid proof of flight-assisted shooting, so they did not meet the core aerial requirement. Third, some works were clearly shot above the permitted flight height, and the authors could not provide valid flight records, which suggests a possible flight violation and does not meet the safety and compliance rules.

The organizing committee has always upheld the principles of fairness, justice, and openness, and it takes the rules of the competition and the authenticity of the works seriously. This handling of violations aims to protect the credibility of the event and the rights of the authors, and to keep the professional quality and reputation of the competition. We thank all sides for their oversight and support. We will continue the work on the winning works and present a high-quality aerial art showcase.
