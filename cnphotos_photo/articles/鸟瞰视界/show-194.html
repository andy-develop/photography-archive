The 5th "Aerial View" National Aerial Photography Competition 2024 was hosted by China Photo Network. The People's Photography Daily, the Entrepreneurs Photography Association (Shenzhen), and many provincial and municipal aerial and tourism photography associations co-organized the event. After more than four months of collecting entries, the competition received works from 8,191 photographers. They came from 31 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities, from Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan, and from countries such as Russia, Singapore, Australia, Malaysia, the United States, and New Zealand. They submitted 73,267 photographs (series) and 511 short videos.
After the deadline, the judges held a first round. They selected about 10 percent of the entries. In the end, 7,310 photographs (series) and 109 short videos entered the final round. The final selection used a rate of about 1.2 percent, as in past years. It chose 1,095 award-winning photographs (series) and 65 short videos across five categories: Urban Landscape, Nature, Humanistic Documentary, Abstract Art, and Short Video. The proposed winners and the full list were shown on China Photo Network platforms for one week, from December 5 to December 12, 2025.

During the public notice period, people from all walks of life and many photographers took part in the review. Their input helped keep the competition fair. The judging committee took reported rule violations seriously. It checked and reviewed each case one by one.

After a strict review, the committee removed the awards of 5 works. The violations fell into three main groups. First, the submitted works had no EXIF data, and the authors could not provide the original files when asked. This did not meet the authenticity standard. Second, the works were actually shot with a DSLR camera. They lacked the bird's-eye view that aerial photography must have. The authors also could not provide proof that an aircraft helped with the shot. This did not meet the core rule for an aerial competition. Third, the shooting height clearly exceeded the flight height limits. The authors could not provide valid flight records. The works may have violated flight rules. This did not meet the safety rules of the competition.

The organizing committee always upholds the principles of fairness, justice, and openness. It takes the rules of the competition and the authenticity of the works seriously. It removed these works to protect the public trust in the competition and the rights of all entrants. It wants to keep the professional quality and reputation of the event. The committee thanks the public for its oversight and support. It will continue the work on the winning works. It will present a high-quality showcase of aerial photography.
