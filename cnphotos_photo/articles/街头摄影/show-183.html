Street Photography View | Between the Real and the Surreal

Published: 2025-11-26 | Category: Street Photography

Chris Hytha is an American photographer known for architectural photography. His strong background in architecture shapes his unique point of view. He uses tools such as drones. He uses new technical methods (for example, stitching many images into an orthographic projection, which is a flat, top-down view). He turns everyday buildings, such as skyscrapers and historic row houses, into visual poems that are both sharp and surreal. His work shows the precision and detail of structures. Through dramatic use of scale, light, and shadow, he gives buildings a life and a story that go beyond reality.
From his 'Rowhomes' series, which records abandoned row houses in Philadelphia, to his 'Highrises' project, which systematically photographs the tops of skyscrapers across the United States, Hytha always focuses on building fragments that people usually overlook. He digs out the marks of time and the cultural stories they hold. He believes art is 'found, not made.' His work reinterprets shared spaces and draws visual meaning from them. His work has won recognition, including the top prize in the Architizer photography competition. Hytha uses his camera to redefine the dialogue between people and the built environment. Between the real and the surreal, he builds one visual bridge after another.
