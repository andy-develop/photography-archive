Street Photography View | Not Aiming at Documentary, but at Poetry

Published: 2025-10-15 | Category: Street Photography

Dimpy Bhalotia is an Indian-born British photographer and a highly recognizable presence in street photography today. She won two awards at the 2020 iPhone Photography Awards. With a dynamic, poetic visual language, she reinterprets the street moments we take for granted.
Bhalotia did not study photography formally. Through self-study and a sharp eye she developed in design, she built a visual style all her own. She excels at catching the "decisive moment" in motion, as in her famous image Flying Boys. Boys leap into the Ganges, their bodies spread as if in flight. The frame holds explosive tension and a deep poetry at once. She loves black and white. Once color is removed, the composition, light, and emotion strike the heart more directly.
From morning bathers in Varanasi, India, to a random glance on a London street, to a lone figure in the Tokyo subway, Bhalotia uses the iPhone as her pen. She writes a visual diary that joins a dynamic aesthetic with minimalism. She does not aim at documentary. She aims at poetry, and she distills moments beyond ordinary reality from the everyday.
She shows us that street photography is no longer a cold record. It is a theater where light, shadow, people, and emotion dance together. As she says, the best camera is the one you carry with you. What truly matters is the way you look at the world.
