Street Photography View | A Realm of Pure Color at Midday

Published: 2025-11-27 | Category: Street Photography

Franco Fontana is a pioneer of Italian color photography. He redefined landscape photography with a highly abstract and minimal visual language. His work strips away the solid feel of objects. Through strong color contrast, geometric structure, and a flattened treatment, he turns real scenes into pure combinations of color and line. This gives the image an abstract quality that is almost like painting.
Fontana likes to shoot in the strong light of midday. He uses a long lens to compress space and avoids shadows. This makes the colors reach their fullest saturation and purity. Since the 1960s, he has used this pioneering style to challenge old ideas about photography. It shaped the aesthetics of color photography and led photography toward more conceptual expression.
In series such as "Presence / Absence" and "Asphalt," Fontana adds philosophical thought. Through shadows and street signs, he explores ideas of presence and absence, of the lasting and the fleeting. He shows a deep reading of the city and the traces people leave. He believes that art aims to make the unseen become seen. His work is the visual practice of this idea.
