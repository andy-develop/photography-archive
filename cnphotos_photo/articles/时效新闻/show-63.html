Guo Guanghui: Silence, Helplessness, and Resilience

Published: 2022-10-25 | Category: News

Chengdu is a city of leisure and comfortable living. People do not want to leave once they arrive. Yet this year Chengdu faced rare troubles. The pandemic kept flaring up. The summer heat was hard to bear. Earthquakes also added to the problem. These events made the usually calm people of Chengdu uneasy. On the last day of August, Chengdu announced major pandemic controls. From September 1, the city ran universal nucleic acid tests for everyone. From 6 p.m. on August 31, residents stayed home in principle. The order caught the people of Chengdu off guard.

Across the city, residents stayed home in principle. People could leave only with a pass, and only to buy daily necessities. Except for supply vehicles, other cars were banned from the streets. Public places such as gyms and libraries closed. Shops such as teahouses and beauty salons shut. Restaurants and snack bars could not serve dine-in guests, and offered only delivery. Factories stopped work. Banks closed. Schools delayed the start of term, and students took online classes at home. Government staff went down to communities to work as volunteers. Residents across the city entered a silent mode.

The main streets were cold and quiet. Supermarkets had few customers. The usual rush of traffic was gone. The warm, everyday feel of the city was missing. Only medical workers and community staff were busy. Only police, delivery couriers, and cleaners kept working. Only supply departments worked in silence. Social life slowed down. The economy eased off. People's daily life grew still. The pandemic cost the economy. It left people feeling helpless.

Yet the people of Chengdu are naturally optimistic and stay calm under change. They show great resilience in hardship. Across the city, people stayed home and carried out the pandemic measures. Everyone who needed a nucleic acid test got one. Party members and residents helped organize controls and served the community. They made sure the whole city got through the hard time.
