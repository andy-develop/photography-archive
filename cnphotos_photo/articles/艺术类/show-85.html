He is a director of the China Art Photography Society and the China Industrial Photography Association, and a board member of the Entrepreneurs Photography Association (Shenzhen). He is a signed photographer for outlets such as People's Photography and Visual China.

Photographic works:

Series of 10, Shaolin Kung Fu Part 1: Chan Wu Kung Fu

Photography is called the art of the moment. Today we are used to reading photography through the instant, and we overlook the many meanings of photographic time. In practice, I feel more and more that the look and spirit of kung fu cannot be fully shown by a single frozen instant. Much of the beauty lies in the time-based process: the real and the empty acting together, the hard and the soft balanced, the fast and the slow alternating, and the opening and closing of a movement. So I returned to the movement itself, and through repeated tests I explored new ways to let time enter the image. By controlling tone, by balancing sharp and soft, and by comparing front-curtain and rear-curtain long exposures, I measured the speed and distance of motion, and set the shutter and exposure. After many trials, I broke through ordinary viewing and description, and reached a kung fu image beyond our visual experience, one whose mood matches what people know and feel about Shaolin kung fu. Chan Wu, the union of Chan and kung fu, is the highest state of Shaolin kung fu. Chan is the soul, and kung fu is its form. Chan trains the mind, and kung fu trains the body. Their union is the essence of Shaolin martial art.

Series of 10, Shaolin Kung Fu Part 2: True Kung Fu

I chose Shaolin kung fu as a long-term project because it has a long history and deep culture. My goal is to use kung fu images to spread Chinese culture and carry forward the Chinese spirit. Because I know the monks, they did not mind my shooting and they helped, so I could photograph top Shaolin masters and their true skill. This adds to the force and the value of the work. This historical and cultural view of the subject asks me to rise beyond the usual personal taste and curiosity of such themes, and to tell the image story from a higher level. As Shaolin kung fu became world famous, it became a Chinese calling card that shines with history and culture. In cultural exchange between China and the world, Shaolin kung fu often appears on the world stage. At home and abroad, it has become a national legend. This lets the folk story of Shaolin kung fu rise into a national story, and it opens a new path for my work. So my work presents the True Kung Fu series. Through these images, I give Shaolin kung fu a meaning beyond itself, and by combining it with other symbols I express the depth and spirit of the nation, so that the image of Shaolin kung fu carries a national view and a higher meaning.

Series of 10, Shaolin Kung Fu Part 3: Imagery Kung Fu

The instant imagery of kung fu focuses on the offensive and defensive forms of Shaolin kung fu. I catch striking moments, change angles, and pull out the most representative moves. With strong visual force, I show the height, skill, strength, and subtlety that go beyond an ordinary body. In the burst of courage, hardness, speed, and power, I raise the force of the image and build the fearless, invincible, tough, and strong image and spirit of Shaolin kung fu. I show masters who stand apart, while also using the setting: red walls and green tiles inside and outside the temple, bamboo and rocks in the hills. In the relation between kung fu and setting, I seek a blend of nature and culture, to show the specific background of Shaolin kung fu and to enrich its meaning. This part shows the main content and key traits of Shaolin kung fu, forms the basic concept of it in image language, and expresses the kung fu imagery in my mind.
