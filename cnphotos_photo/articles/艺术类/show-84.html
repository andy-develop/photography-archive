Photographic works:

20-photo series, The Song Tombs in Time and Space

The Yellow River nurtured a rich and splendid Chinese civilization. The culture of the Chinese people evolved over thousands of years and reached its peak in the Song Dynasty. The Northern Song imperial tombs on the south bank of the Yellow River, north of Mount Song, have weathered a thousand years. The stone statues that have stood for a millennium watch a new era across the ruins of history. For years, I traveled through nine provinces of the Yellow River basin to shoot the series Watching Over the Mother River. In the 2011 wheat harvest, when I first came to the Song Tombs, the lifelike stone figures and the farmers harvesting in the sweat stayed in my mind. Over more than ten years, I came to the Song Tombs dozens of times. I kept searching for a way to express the weight of history and culture, the people who have watched over it for a thousand years, and the new era as time turns. Through artistic images, I try to show the cultural confidence of a vast China, the enjoyment of modern civilization, and the longing for poetry and the distance. I come here again and again, to hear you tell the story of a nation, the story of a country and its family. In the grand and vast expanse, clouds have swept, winds have swept, and history has swept. You stand in the field, between the sun and the earth. You have stood a thousand years in the wild, amid the height of the mountains and the breadth of the river. Through the turning of the stars and the change of the world, you gaze from the ruins of history toward a new era.

10-photo series, Khoomei

The wind sways, the flowers fall like snow, and the vast, boundless song is the khoomei of the distant land. The voice of nature, the soaring sound of heaven, has rung out for a thousand years. The sky is blue, the pasture is green, and clouds drift across the wide grassland. In the age of ten thousand horses running, another spring has come.
