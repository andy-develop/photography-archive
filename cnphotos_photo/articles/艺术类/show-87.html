Series of 10 photos: Wet-Plate Ice Flowers

This entry has three themes, from "Winter of the Derung People" and "Love of Half a Century" to the classical wet-plate work "The Past and Present of Wet-Plate Ice Flowers." The time span starts in 1989, when I entered the Derung River valley, then runs through 25 years as I looked for "love of half a century" among the many ethnic groups of China. Finally, during the three years of the COVID-19 pandemic, I shot the wet-plate series "Wet Plate and Ice Flowers." The three projects together took 35 years. "Winter of the Derung People" was finished in 1989. At that time, documentary photography was rising in Taiwan. A newspaper article of only a few hundred words told how a horse caravan, cut off by heavy snow, carried winter supplies to the Derung people, who had been cut off from the world for half a year. It deeply moved me. On the 1989 trip to the Derung River, I was blocked by landslides along the way. As the caravan crossed the snow line of the Gaoligong Mountains again and again, I photographed the last horse caravans. Like an ascetic monk, they went through hardship, and it gave this trip a real, flesh-and-blood story. The way I showed the Derung people was quite advanced for its time, both in idea and in the images themselves. "Love of Half a Century" took a very long time. It began in 1991 and was shown at the China Millennium Monument in Beijing in 2016. Over a quarter century, I visited more than 300 couples who had been married for over 50 years. I chose the environmental portraits of 99 golden-wedding couples, from ethnic minorities and many groups on both sides of the Taiwan Strait. By then, documentary photography on both sides had moved to a post-documentary stage. While I spent 25 years on "Love of Half a Century," I also worked long-term on documentary themes of ethnic minorities. To keep up with the times and add new elements to my documentary work, I then started from zero to learn the classical "wet-plate collodion" process. During the COVID-19 pandemic, I put all my time into wet-plate work. From the failed, discarded plates, I discovered ice flowers, a strange thing. The discovery of "ice flowers" came after long observation and practice. Wet-plate photography has a special tone. In the first half century after photography was invented, it left many classic images. Today, more people use wet plate for creative work. The challenge is how to break through the classic image, not just repeat it. By chance, from a discarded, failed plate, I found ice flowers. This magic appears only from this kind of mistake. After that, once I mastered the element of wet-plate ice flowers, I found my entry point. The formation of wet-plate ice flowers is hard to control. After steady effort, I finally solved the chance of ice-flower formation. I could adjust the time when the ice flowers form and control how fast the crystals grow. It was like finding the door to the mystery of ice flowers. Inside is a wide space to work.
