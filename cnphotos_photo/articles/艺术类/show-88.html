Photography experience:

She works at the School of Art, Dalian Medical University. She does research in medical photography and teaches photography.

Photographic works:

Six-photo series "States of Life - Dissolution"

This work joins traditional images (Polaroid) with chemical breakdown. It records and expresses scenes, stories, and the colorful life across long spans of time and space. The melted "images" are no longer a mirror-like reflection or record. In the erosion of time and technique, they release life and time again. Through them, I record a 20-year period of practice: one object, one moment, is one life.

Four-photo series "States of Life - Dialogue"

Today, with the help of technology, science translates biological structures into "images". Photographic art uses technique to show its understanding of life. While both care about life and health, the work builds a shared structure between humanist ideas and scientific knowledge. In this series, I write directly on the images. I write my dialogue with the subjects. I write the science facts I know about them. I write about the time I spent with them. Through this way of feeling and remembering, the work shows a chaotic realm where the world meets the vast universe. It asks what the essence of life is.

Ten-photo series "States of Life - Resurrection"

These animals once had their own miracles of life. In the images, they look like objects from another world in their setting, abrupt and absurd. The work expresses the relationship between animals, humans, and nature in social development. It also shows reverence and love for nature and life. It carries a reflection on science, technology, and art. Science seeks truth. The humanities speak of goodness. Art turns toward beauty. Scientists use truth to speak of beauty. I want to use the eyes of science and the means of photography to sense and think about the meaning of life.

Ten-photo series "States of Life - Theater"

This part uses black-and-white images to record the final poses of different animals in the sky, on land, and in the sea. It freezes and interprets the final moment of life. Each photo is like a single still from a film. I use photography to trace how the animals looked when they were alive, and to show the immortality of life. After I organize and archive these images, I use the recording power of images to build a visual archive with educational value. I also explore the visual language that comes from joining medicine and photographic art.
