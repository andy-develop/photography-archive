Liu Xinwu: Work by a Prospective Winner of the 14th China Photography Gold Statue Award — Documentary Category

Published: 2023-05-12 | Category: Documentary

July 9, 2021. As the Communist Party of China marked its centenary, visitors kept coming to the newly built Beijing Memorial Hall of the Communist Party of China.

September 13, 2018. Children at a kindergarten in Guangzhou, Guangdong, rehearsed the Guangdong lion dance in costume. It was one project of the kindergarten's intangible cultural heritage in schools program.

May 7, 2018. A technician worked on the production line of a high-tech company in the Yingtan High-tech Zone, Jiangxi. Over those years, the zone built up the internet of things and related strategic emerging industries such as 5G and smart manufacturing, aiming to become a new highland for high-tech industry.

October 22, 2014. Children practiced shooting hoops at the new central kindergarten in Dawang Town, Guangrao County, Shandong. That year, the Ministry of Education pushed for the full implementation of Central Document No. 7 and General Office Document No. 53, which required local schools to strengthen physical education and extracurricular exercise and promote the well-rounded development of students.

August 23, 2013. Datong, Shanxi, held a fashion show on a restored Ming Dynasty city wall. In 2009, Datong began an eight-year project to restore its ancient city walls.

May 18, 2012. Pupils at Guangwushan Town Primary School in Nanjiang County, Bazhong, Sichuan, ate a free nutritious breakfast. That year, the province launched a nutrition improvement plan for rural compulsory education students, giving a daily subsidy of 3 yuan per rural student.

May 17, 2009. Residents and tourists posed with the World Expo mascot in a Shikumen lane in Shanghai. On April 30 of the next year, the 41st World Expo opened in Shanghai. It was the first World Expo hosted by China.

May 19, 2007. Doctors at a hospital in Zibo, Shandong, adjusted newly imported medical equipment. To better meet people's medical needs, many local health departments worked to bring in advanced diagnostic equipment and steadily raise the quality of care.

February 12, 2007. The Wulihe Stadium in Shenyang, Liaoning, built in 1988, was demolished by controlled blast at 12:15 that day. On October 7, 2001, the Chinese men's national football team qualified here and reached the World Cup for the first time.

November 21, 2004. Troops of the Yongxing Island coastal defense station in the Xisha Islands used coconuts and training grenades as equipment for a fun bowling game. Conditions were tough, but the mood was warm.

May 12, 1995. An unprecedented science-popularization event opened in Beijing. It made the clear point that mastering the computer is the ID card of the talent of the 21st century.

November 22, 1994. The world-famous violinist and Grammy winner Itzhak Perlman visited the Central Conservatory of Music in Beijing at its invitation. During his stay, he performed for and taught the students and teachers.

January 28, 1994. The noted singer Guo Lanying, in tears, embraced a child out of school during a performance. That day, the Ministry of Culture and the Ministry of Radio, Film and Television held a large charity show for Project Hope at the Great Hall of the People in Beijing.

Winter 1993. Beijing industry and commerce and public security departments jointly held a campaign to destroy counterfeit goods. In an open field on the edge of the city, many fake products, including counterfeit cigarettes and liquor, were burned.

October 1993. A group of children, with their parents, prepared for a dance grading exam. At the time, the Beijing Dance Academy grading center was the first institution in China, approved by the Ministry of Culture, to offer high-level general dance education and grading exams.

Autumn 1993. An in-house trench coat design show at the Beijing Great Wall Trench Coat Company. The company was a well-known state-owned enterprise of its time, famous for its distinctive designs, and its products were all the rage.

Early 1993. The German composer Carsten Gudeman rehearsed the Peking opera The Nightingale at the National Academy of Chinese Theatre Arts. At the end of that year, the opera, written and composed by him and performed by teachers and students of the academy and adapted from the tale by the Danish author Hans Christian Andersen, opened at the Cultural Palace of Nationalities in Beijing.

Summer 1992. The geneticist Tan Jiazhen and five other scientists, accompanied by their wives, visited Taiwan by way of Hong Kong. It was the first time mainland scientists set foot on the island after the founding of the People's Republic.

End of December 1991. The piano department of the Central Conservatory of Music in Beijing held a New Year event. Masters such as the noted pianist Liu Shikun gathered with the students in a classroom.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 1: Academician Chen Xingshen (October 28, 1911 to December 3, 2004), one of the greatest geometers of the 20th century, known as the father of global differential geometry.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 2: Academician Qian Xuesen (December 11, 1911 to October 31, 2009), an aerodynamicist and systems scientist, a founder of engineering cybernetics, and a recipient of the Two Bombs, One Satellite merit medal.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 3: Academician Hou Xianglin (April 4, 1912 to December 8, 2008), a Chinese chemical engineer, an expert in fuel technology, a founder of China's refining technology, and a pioneer of petrochemical technology.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 4: Academician Qian Weichang (October 9, 1912 to July 30, 2010), a world-renowned scientist and educator and an outstanding public figure, with major achievements in elasticity, the calculus of variations, and perturbation methods.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 5: Academician Wang Shizhen (March 7, 1916 to May 27, 2016), a biochemist and a specialist in nuclear medicine.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 6: the academician couple. Academician Zhou Tingchong (left, March 6, 1917 to October 20, 1996), a biochemical pharmacologist. Academician Huang Cuifen (March 6, 1921 to August 9, 2011), an expert in microbiology, immunology, and genetic engineering.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 7: Academician Wu Wenjun (May 12, 1919 to May 7, 2017), a mathematician, a recipient of the Tan Kah Kee Science Award, and a winner of the 2000 State Preeminent Science and Technology Award.

February 14, 1993. The Thinker, by the world-famous sculptor Rodin, arrived at the National Art Museum of China in Beijing. The next day, the large French Rodin art exhibition opened there.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 8: Academician Shi Changxu (November 15, 1920 to November 10, 2014), a metallurgist and materials scientist and a strategic scientist, who worked for decades on superalloys, alloy steel, and metal corrosion and protection.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 9: Academician Wu Liangyong, born May 1922, an architect, an urban and regional planner, and an educator, who worked for decades on architecture education and on the theory and practice of urban planning and design. He received the 2011 State Preeminent Science and Technology Award.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 10: Academician Yang Zhenning, born October 1, 1922, a physicist and a Nobel laureate in physics, named a Touching China Person of the Year for 2021 in 2022.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 11: Academician Qian Zhengying, born July 4, 1923, an expert in water resources and hydropower, who received the 2014 Guanghua Engineering Science and Technology Achievement Award.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 12: Academician Zhu Guangya (December 25, 1924 to February 26, 2011), a recipient of the Two Bombs, One Satellite merit medal, and a Touching China Person of the Year for 2011.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 13: Academician Zhang Cunhao, born February 23, 1928, a physical chemist and laser chemist, a founder of high-energy chemical lasers in China and a founder of molecular reaction dynamics, and a recipient of the State Preeminent Science and Technology Award.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 14: Academician Zhou Guangzhao, born May 15, 1929, a theoretical and particle physicist. In 1996, the minor planet number 3462 was named Zhouguangzhao in his honor.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 15: Academician Yuan Longping (September 7, 1930 to May 22, 2021), a noted agricultural scientist and the founder and leader of hybrid rice in China, a recipient of the Medal of the Republic, and known as the father of hybrid rice.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 16: Academician Li Fanghua (January 6, 1932 to January 24, 2020), a noted physicist and electron microscopy expert and one of the pioneers of electron diffraction and high-resolution electron microscopy in China. She received the 2003 L'Oreal-UNESCO For Women in Science award.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 17: Academician Qin Boyi, born November 6, 1932, a pharmacologist who worked for decades on neuro-psychopharmacology and toxicology.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 18: Academician Zhong Nanshan, born October 20, 1936, a respiratory physician and the leading figure in China's fight against SARS and COVID-19. On August 11, 2020, he received the Medal of the Republic.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 19: Academician Wang Xuan (February 5, 1937 to February 13, 2006), an expert in computer text information processing, the founder of Chinese-character laser typesetting, and a recipient of the State Preeminent Science and Technology Award.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 20: Academician Jiang Boju, born September 4, 1937, a mathematician and topologist who worked for decades on fixed-point theory and low-dimensional topology.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 21: Academician Niu Wenyuan (November 1937 to September 28, 2016), head and chief scientist of the sustainable development strategy group at the Chinese Academy of Sciences. He won one National Invention Award and one first-class Science and Technology Progress Award of the CAS.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 22: Academician Chen Jiansheng, born July 1938, an astrophysicist who mainly studies theoretical astrophysics, including quasar surveys, quasar absorption lines, and the intergalactic medium in large-scale, large-sample astronomy.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 23: Academician Xia Jianbai, born 1939, a semiconductor physicist whose main field is semiconductor theory, including electronic states in semiconductor microstructures, magnetic semiconductors and spintronics, and the generation, transport, and relaxation of quantum coherent states.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 24: Academician Wei Yu, born February 7, 1940, an electronics expert who worked for decades on the theory and application of microwave electronics, bioelectronics, and molecular electronics.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 25: Academician Lu Yongxiang, born April 28, 1942, an expert in fluid power transmission and control, who worked for decades on fluid transmission and control technology.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 26: Academician Xu Zhihong, born October 1942, a plant physiologist who worked for decades on plant developmental biology, plant cell culture, and plant bioengineering.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 27: Academician Zhang Boli, born February 26, 1948, who received a first-class National Science and Technology Progress Award in 2014 and the national honorary title of People's Hero in August 2020.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 28: Academician Wu Yiling, born October 24, 1949, a specialist in traditional Chinese medicine for cardiovascular disease, skilled in the TCM collateral-disease theory and in treating coronary heart disease, arrhythmia, chronic heart failure, and stroke.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 29: Academician Wang Shicheng, born January 1953, a mathematician who worked for decades on low-dimensional topology, dynamical systems, and algebraic topology. He received awards such as the China Young Scientist Award and the Shiing-Shen Chern Mathematics Prize.

Portraits of Academicians, no. 30: Academician Wang Chen, born August 1962, a specialist in pulmonology and critical care medicine, who has worked for decades on clinical care, teaching, and research in respiratory medicine.

30-photo series, Portraits of Academicians

Academicians of the Chinese Academy of Sciences and the Chinese Academy of Engineering, known together as the two academies, hold the highest honor a scientist in China can receive. Because I work in news photography, they also became the main subjects of my camera. I covered the 1992 academic meeting for the 10th anniversary of the National Natural Science Foundation of China, and the third general assembly of the Chinese Academy of Engineering in 1996. During those events, I recorded many academicians as they talked with one another, took careful notes, spoke freely, or thought in deep concentration. In that era, when a camera was still a luxury, I was the only photographer on site. So these images became exclusive records and are all the more precious. Over the years, I photographed nearly a hundred academicians. Some are famous at home and abroad, and some are little known. But each was a pioneer in their field. They are a treasure of the nation, the pride of the people, and the glory of the nation, and they are masters I deeply admire. Some have since passed away, and others still work hard in their fields. I am deeply gratified that I had the chance to preserve their images, so that we can still see their dignified bearing today.
