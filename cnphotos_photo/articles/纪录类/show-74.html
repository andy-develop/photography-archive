Photographs:

Series of 10: Football Kingdom - Brazil

Football is the national sport of Brazil. Brazil has won the World Cup five times. It is no exaggeration to say football runs in Brazilian blood. The whole nation is crazy about the game. Most babies get a football as their first gift. Every family member supports a club, and relatives argue over their favorite stars. When Brazil plays in the World Cup, the president orders the country to watch. The whole nation stops work to cheer. This series shows, from many angles, how deeply football is part of Brazilian identity.

Series of 15: Cowboys on Horseback

Parts of northeastern Brazil are dry all year. Dead trees and brush cover the land, and the terrain is unique. Over a century, this region raised cattle and a rare class of cowboys. In the past, most Brazilian cowboys worked for large ranch owners. Today they are mostly family-based groups. Over generations, they built their own plain customs and a cowboy festival. The passionate songs of the folk singer Luis Gonzaga made Brazilian society know its cowboys. Dressed in leather pants and jackets and leather helmets, the cowboys developed grit and courage through hard times. Their honest image is admired, and their story reached the screen. I decided to enter their lives, experience their daily world, make friends, and hear their stories. I wanted to show a real, living group and their simple details and joys. I drove more than 5,000 kilometers across four states and stayed with the cowboys many times to finish this series.

Themed series of 25: Two Decades of China-Brazil Friendship: BRICS Cooperation on the Belt and Road

This year marks ten years since China and Brazil set up a comprehensive strategic partnership. In this decade, trade, cultural, and people-to-people exchange reached a level never seen since ties began. This series shows, over more than 20 years in Brazil, details of everyday life and the cultural exchanges that bring the two peoples closer. In recent years, more and more goods in Brazilian daily life come from China. Chinese-made subway cars, construction machinery, and power equipment have entered Brazil. Even carnival decorations and fishing gear come from China. In return, Brazilian tropical juice, soybeans, meat, and coffee sell well in China. This shows deep fit across many fields. BRICS cooperation in recent years has lifted China-Brazil ties to a new level, with broader scope. The relationship will grow closer. I believe that photographing the everyday life and cultural exchange of the two peoples is meaningful work for friendship.
