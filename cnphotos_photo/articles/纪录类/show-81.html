1. Each season, the international fashion week brings together many top designers. It also selects the annual winners of the top model award in China. So model agencies and models from all over the country come. The big open casting of fashion week has become the largest and most concentrated model casting in the country.

2. The big casting matters a lot to every fashion model. So each model gives their best. They hope more designers will pick them, and that the casting will bring more shows.

3. March 5, 2021, 751D Park, Beijing. This was the first open casting of China International Fashion Week in Beijing since the COVID-19 outbreak. It drew more than 3,000 Chinese and foreign models from all over the country. For a fashion week that had been offline for a year, it drew wide attention.

4. Every fashion week in China brings together many top clothing designers. They grow on this big stage and build their careers on it. They play a key role in the development of Chinese fashion. The photo shows Wang Yutao, a famous Chinese designer and winner of the 15th and 21st Golden Top Awards for Chinese fashion design, preparing for his brand show at the 2021 spring fashion week. He has shown his new collections at China International Fashion Week for many years.

5. Pan Yiliang, a well-known Taiwanese fashion designer, has taken part in China International Fashion Week for 8 years. On this stage, her design skills keep improving. The Chinese fashion world has recognized her, and she won the Golden Top Award, the highest prize in Chinese fashion design, in 2017. She values this stage deeply and prepares every show by herself.

6. Ji Wenbo is a master of Chinese fashion design. He has won both the Chinese Golden Top Award and the Milan Golden Top Award in Italy. For his big show at China International Fashion Week in April 2021, he personally checked every step. The photo shows a fitting and runway rehearsal.

7. Makeup is a key part of the show and cannot be ignored. In December 2020, at the Quanzhou Haute Couture International Fashion Week in Jinan, a model in the famous designer Guo Pei couture show spent over an hour on her headpiece, so it would match the haute couture garments.

8. Ma Yanli, a famous Chinese model and fashion designer, coaches a young Tibetan model who is in Beijing for the first time and walking a runway for the first time, during rehearsal for her own show.

9. April 16, 2021, Shenzhen Fashion Week, Guangdong. A model talks with the designer during rehearsal about a problem with her runway outfit.

10. Designers focus on rehearsal and give direction, so the models show their clothes in the right style. The photo shows master designer Ji Wenbo coaching a model runway walk on the T stage to achieve an elegant, noble effect.

11. At China International Fashion Week, the shows follow one another tightly, so backstage is very busy. The photo shows a makeup artist and a costume designer giving last-minute touch-ups and fitting accessories to a model who is about to walk.

12. A model in the famous domestic and international designer Guo Pei couture show wears a complex outfit. After rehearsal she can only stand and wait in full costume. The long wait leaves her very tired. She hopes the dresser will come soon and find a way to ease her load.

13. March 26, 2018. After the "Mao Geping Makeup" show at China International Fashion Week, the famous Chinese actress Liu Xiaoqing came on stage to give flowers and pose with Mao Geping and his team.

14. November 21, 2019. Yumi Katsura, the 87-year-old Japanese national treasure of haute couture and the world queen of wedding dresses, presented her signature couture gowns, dresses, and kimono at the closing of Quancheng Style International Fashion Week. The beauty of traditional Japanese culture met French gradient dyeing, giving the audience a visual feast.

15. December 7, 2020. The three-day 2020 Quancheng Style International Fashion Week closed. This edition invited haute couture masters from China, South Korea, and Japan. Because of COVID-19, international designers from abroad could not attend. The closing show missed the shot of famous Chinese and foreign couture designers taking the stage together.

16. April 19, 2021. Hong Kong star Christy Chung joined the final show of this Shenzhen Fashion Week. Today it is normal for designers and brands to invite celebrities to walk, and for stars, influencers, and notable guests to attend.

17. Children couture gowns have grown fast in recent years. At China International Fashion Week in September 2021, Lu Fanghua, designer of the Honggu Xiaoai brand, wove traditional Song brocade into that season children gowns. The result gives children couture a unique look.

18. September 11, 2021. China International Fashion Week held a show that brought together Eastern roots and Western charm. The famous designer Ji Wenbo used the theme "Dong XI". He drew inspiration from the form, spirit, color, and mood of Dunhuang and Thangka culture. Abstract patterns met commercial fashion, and ethnic style took on a world character.

19. Three months later, Ji Wenbo, the first designer to win both the Chinese and Italian Golden Top Awards, brought another surprise to China International Fashion Week. With his JIWENBO PRIVE brand, he offered the Chinese fashion design field a couture collection that blends fashion and classic, Eastern culture and Western aesthetics. The collection draws on more than 40 years of his design work. It moved people with Chinese design once again.

20. China International Fashion Week 2021 closed in a feast of style. The photo shows Ji Wenbo taking the curtain call with all the models.

1. After China joined the WTO, trade and industry exchanges in textiles grew. To promote the industry and expand the Chinese market, the China International Fabric and Accessories Expo moved from Beijing to Shanghai in 2004. Its first edition drew huge attention. The international hall was packed. High-quality foreign fabrics gave Chinese textiles many ideas. The crowds also showed foreign firms the huge potential of the Chinese fabric market.

2. In 2000, the Chinese Textile and Apparel Trade Show opened in New York. After 10 years of promotion, Chinese textiles and apparel opened markets in the United States and nearby. It became China most influential textile show in the US and won support and recognition from local government.

3. In 2014, the Xianyang Textile Park in Shaanxi opened. The old Northwest No.1 Cotton Mill left its 50-year-old plant and equipment and moved into the park. After months of relocation and equipment setup, Shaanxi No.1 Cotton Mill went into full production in 2015. A master craftsman about to retire maintains the spinning frames in the new workshop, standing his last watch.

4. In October 2014, after the Chinese textile and apparel shows in the US and Paris, the China Textile and Apparel Exhibition opened in Brazil. It was the first Chinese textile show in South America. Its first edition drew many buyers.

5. Wuxi No.1 Cotton Group was founded in 1919. Its predecessor was Shenxin No.3 Mill, started by the patriotic industrialists Rong Zongjing and Rong Desheng. It was once a model of Chinese national industry. In 2019, for the group centenary, Rong Zhijian, grandson of the founder, was invited to visit. He showed great interest in the modern workshops and praised the changes and growth of the 100-year-old mill.

6. October 16, 2019. For the centenary, Wuxi No.1 Cotton linked by CCTV to its factory in Ethiopia and launched production there. After decades of effort, Chinese textiles have grown from a large producer into a strong textile nation. More and more Chinese woven goods appear around the world.

7. China wool textile industry started late but grew fast after 2000. Sunshine Group is a leading Chinese wool textile company. Its national-level technology innovation system develops more than 50 new fabrics a day on average, putting it at the top of the industry, with products at an advanced international level. The ceremonial dress of the PLA honor guard, and ceremonial dress for large events and the military, all come from Sunshine. The photo shows a corner of Sunshine high-precision workshop.

In the late 1950s, Zhao Mengtao of the Northwest National Cotton Mill No.1 won the national model worker award twice and was held up as a red banner of the national textile front. Sixty years later, in the Zhao Mengtao team at Xianyang Textile Group No.1 Mill (the former Northwest No.1), the spirit of high standards, strict demands, fast action, solid work, taking on hardship, and making things easy for others, lives on and passes from generation to generation.

9. June 11, 2021. The China International Textile Machinery Expo opened at the National Exhibition Center in Shanghai. Despite COVID-19, the halls were still crowded. As Chinese textiles grew stronger, Chinese textile machinery makers kept improving their technology. Many domestic booths were packed, and new technology and equipment drew the most attention.

10. Chinese textiles have come a long way in a century, from nothing, to clothing the Chinese people, to a world textile power. The industry has gone through several waves of change. Today Chinese textiles are known worldwide, and made-in-China reaches every corner. This is the work and creation of generation after generation of textile workers. The photo shows a female textile worker at Xianyang Textile Group watching a loom and checking the cloth quality.

1. In the early 1950s, Northwest No.1 Cotton Mill was the first to settle in the Xianyang "textile city". It became a new force in Chinese textiles and did much to solve the clothing shortage for ordinary Chinese. Today it is the last cotton mill to leave the "textile city" in Xianyang, and in all of China.

2. In the abandoned plant, you can still see the marks of the five new state-owned cotton textile bases of the 1950s: the same stepped factory buildings and T-shaped girders. Even the clock hanging in the middle of the workshop is identical.

3. Small blackboards are everywhere in the workshop. Each lists the duties of a technical post. Beside them are the workers simple storage lockers.

4. Row after row of equipment is the first generation of domestic spinning frames. From how densely they stand, you can imagine how busy this place once was.

5. The rusty motor and the old push-button switches tell their own history. The nameplate marks its date of issue: 19695.

6. The shift-length record for the three-shift system shows not only the working hours of textile workers then, but also the drive to outdo one another.

7. The four comparison pictures are a small sample of how the cotton textile industry raised output and managed quality back then.

8. The big headline on the workshop blackboard shows the confidence and strong sense of duty of the Zhao Mengtao team that once worked here.

9. During the 12th Five-Year Plan, new textile industrial parks rose one after another. The old cotton textile bases slowly disappeared, and the once-admired cotton mills became a memory of history. With concentrated scale and automated production, Chinese cotton textile has entered a new era. The photo shows the fully automatic carding machines at Wuxi No.1 Cotton Textile Group.

10. In 2018, Northwest No.1 Cotton Mill left its 60-year-old site and moved into the Xianyang Textile Industrial Park. It began a new journey. With new equipment, new technology, and new processes, it carries forward the Mengtao spirit in the new setting and works hard to realize the Chinese Dream.

Name: Cui Xiaoman

Gender: Female

Ethnicity: Han Chinese

Date of birth: 1957-01-10

Political status: Public citizen

Education: Junior college

Employer: Organizing Committee of the "Great National Heavyweights" Industrial Photography Art Exhibition

Position: Secretary-General

Title: Associate senior, associate deputy editor

Native place: Gaoyou City, Yangzhou, Jiangsu Province

Photography career:

She was assistant to the president and executive vice president of the China Textile Photography Association. She has long worked on photography about the textile industry.

Works:

Series of 10 images "Fashion Portraits"

As Chinese textile industry has progressed, Chinese textiles and apparel have moved from self-sufficiency to becoming a major clothing producer. More people are no longer satisfied with simple clothing. The demand for fashion and individual style, together with market competition, has brought one fashion week after another. It has also pushed Chinese fashion design away from old-fashioned ideas toward its own innovation. In the past ten years, the low-end shows on Chinese runways have decreased. There are more garments that draw on ethnic culture, more haute couture, and more creative, individual designs. Chinese designers such as Ji Wenbo and Guo Pei have taken the international fashion stage. The Chinese runway has become a series of fashion landmarks. Fashion Runway points its lens at the front and back of the fashion week. It records famous designers at work on the runway and the effort models put into the shows. Through these small details, it tells the changes and stories of the Chinese runway over ten years.

Series of 10 images "Textile Notes"

Since the reform and opening up, Chinese textile industry rose quickly after several waves of change. This series uses images of several representative Chinese textile and apparel shows held abroad after China joined the WTO. It shows how Chinese textiles and apparel reached the world step by step and grew their share of international markets. It uses moments from the centenary of Wuxi No.1 Cotton Group to show the change and growth of Chinese textiles. It uses the crowded Chinese booth at the China International Textile Machinery Show to show how Chinese textile machinery moved from imported equipment to independent development, and became a pillar of Chinese textile growth. It uses modern female textile workers who strive for excellence to show the new generation carrying on tradition and serving with dedication. This is a small record of Chinese textile development, and a scene in the story of China becoming a world textile power.

Series of 10 images "Textile Memory"

In the early years of New China, Chinese textile industry was very backward. The textile mills left in Shanghai, Qingdao, and Tianjin from the colonial period dragged old equipment and shouldered the task of clothing hundreds of millions of people. To solve the clothing shortage, the government built cotton mills in Beijing, Zhengzhou, Shijiazhuang, Handan, and Xianyang, and used labor contests to raise output. This slowly eased the clothing shortage. Today the shortage is long past. The mills where machines once roared have been replaced one by one by new industrial parks. Who can picture the struggle of that generation? This series uses a few representative details: the buildings from the same Soviet blueprint, the large clock hanging in the workshop, the machine nameplates, and the numbers on the shift record. It shows the working environment and atmosphere of textile workers then. It hopes these images bring back the spirit of that generation who worked for the country and the people.
