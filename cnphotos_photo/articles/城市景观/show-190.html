The 5th 2024 "Bird's-eye View" National Aerial Photography Contest is hosted by China Photoforum. It is co-organized by People's Photography, the Entrepreneurs Photographic Association (Shenzhen), the Henan Aerial Photography Association, the Hebei Aerial Photography Society, the Anhui Aerial Flight Brigade, the Tianjin Culture and Tourism Photography Association, the Zhejiang Culture and Tourism Photography Association, the Liaoning Tourism Photography Association, the Jiangxi Tourism Photography Association, the Shaanxi Tourism Photography Association, the Fujian Tourism Photography Association, the Hubei Tourism Photography Association, the Hebei Culture and Tourism Photography Society, the Hong Kong-Shenzhen-Zhuhai-Macau Photography Club, and the Hangzhou Photographers Association Aerial Branch. After more than four months of calls for entries, it received 73,267 photos (sets) and 511 short videos. They came from 8,191 photographers in 31 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities, in Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan, and in Russia, Singapore, Australia, Malaysia, the United States, and New Zealand.
After the deadline, a first review selected entries at about 10 percent. In the end, 7,310 photos (sets) and 109 short videos entered the final review. The final review used the past rate of about 1.2 percent. It chose 1,095 photos (sets) and 65 short videos for graded awards and excellence awards across five categories: urban landscape, nature, humanistic documentary, abstract art, and short video. The proposed winners and the list were posted for a week of public notice from December 5 to December 12, 2025, on all China Photoforum platforms.

During the notice period, people from all sides and many photographers took part in oversight. This gave important support to a fair and just contest. The review committee took seriously the rule problems that people reported. It checked each case carefully and reviewed it.

After strict checks, the contest removed 5 works from the winners. The rule problems fell into three groups. First, the entries had no EXIF data, and the author could not provide the original file during the file check. This broke the truth standard for entries. Second, a work was shot with a single-lens reflex camera. It lacked the bird's-eye view that an aerial shot must have, and the author could not show proof of an aircraft-assisted shot. This broke the core rule of an "aerial" contest. Third, a work was shot well above the legal flight height, and the author could not show valid flight filing data. It may have been an illegal flight, which breaks the safety rule.

The contest committee keeps to the principles of fairness, justice, and openness. It takes contest rules and entry truth seriously. This action protects the trust in the contest and the rights of all entrants. It keeps the professional quality and the good name of the contest. We thank all sides for their oversight and support. We will keep working on the winners, and we will present a high-quality aerial show.
